## 1. Principal books and texts

### 1. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (2020, compiled by Eric Jorgenson)

**Nature:** Eric Jorgenson selected and compiled material from a decade of Naval's Twitter posts, podcasts, and essays. Naval authorized and endorsed the book, and the entire text is available as a free download.

**Structure:** Two main parts.
- **Part I: Wealth** — How to create wealth beyond earning money.
- **Part II: Happiness** — How to learn happiness instead of chasing it.

**Core framework:**
> "Wealth and happiness are both skills you can learn."

---

### 2. "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" Tweetstorm (May 31, 2018)

**Nature:** Naval published a series of 39 tweets on Twitter. His most widely shared work later became a series of articles at nav.al and appeared in the Almanack.

**Naval's own account:**
> "It's not really about money or luck. It's about becoming the kind of person that makes money."

**Core structure of the 39 tweets, reorganized by logical layer:**

**Layer 1: Distinguish concepts**
- "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep. Money is how we transfer time and wealth. Status is your place in the social hierarchy."
- "Understand that ethical wealth creation is possible. If you secretly despise wealth, it will elude you."

**Layer 2: Do not sell your time**
- "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a business — to gain your financial freedom."

**Layer 3: Wealth formula = specific knowledge + leverage + judgment**
- "Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage."

**Layer 4: Escape competition**
- "Escape competition through authenticity."

**Layer 5: Long-term thinking**
- "Play long-term games with long-term people. All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest."

**Layer 6: Permissionless leverage**
- "Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich. You can create software and media that works for you while you sleep."

### 3. "Life Formulas I" (2008 blog post, nav.al)

**Nature:** An early blog post that compresses Naval's framework for life into formulas. This is his earliest public expression of systematic thinking, a decade before the tweetstorm.

**Core formulas:**
```
Happiness = Health + Wealth + Good Relationships
Health = Exercise + Diet + Sleep
Wealth = Income + Wealth * (Return on Investment)
Income = Accountability + Leverage + Specific Knowledge
```

**Key insights:**
> "The three big ones in life are wealth, health, and happiness. We pursue them in that order, but their importance is reverse."

> "All the real benefits in life come from compound interest."

> "Earn with your mind, not your time."

> "Truth is that which has predictive power."

**Significance for his intellectual development:** The 2008 post already contained early forms of specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability. They were the product of a decade of thought, not ideas he suddenly came up with in 2018.

### 4. The Venture Hacks blog (from 2007, written with Babak Nivi)

**Nature:** A blog Naval wrote with AngelList co-founder Nivi. It focused on the game theory of startup financing, especially on how to negotiate investment terms.

**Main themes:**
- Analyze venture capital terms with game theory.
- Help founders take the initiative in fundraising.
- Remove intermediaries so founders can reach angel investors directly.

**Evolution:** Venture Hacks blog → mailing list recommending deals to 25 angel investors → AngelList platform (2010).

**Intellectual significance:** Naval was already applying ideas he later described as permissionless leverage, disintermediation, and democratizing information. He turned dissatisfaction with information asymmetry in venture capital into a product.

### 6. The Sovereign Child (2025, presented in the source notes as co-authored with Aaron Stupple)

**Nature:** The notes describe it as Naval's new book on parenting philosophy, drawing on Karl Popper and David Deutsch's Taking Children Seriously movement. It was first discussed publicly on the Tim Ferriss podcast in January 2025.

**Core ideas:**
- Treat children as equal creators of knowledge rather than as people to control.
- Prefer self-directed learning to structured curricula.
- Prefer self-regulation to strict discipline.
- In practice, avoid setting a fixed daily schedule, allow free choice of food, and let interests guide learning.

**Intellectual significance:** This is Naval's application of Popper's falsificationism and Deutsch's theory of knowledge creation to parenting. His five children are his testing ground.

### 7. "44 Harsh Truths About Human Nature" (March 2025, Modern Wisdom podcast #922)

**Nature:** Naval's latest in-depth interview with Chris Williamson on Modern Wisdom, lasting more than three hours and released March 31, 2025.

**Important new points:**
- Naval publicly revised some earlier statements:
  > "I made that statement a long time ago, and a lot of these things are just notes to myself and they're highly contextual."

- On the tension between happiness and success:
  > He previously said "Happiness is being satisfied with what you have. Success comes from dissatisfaction."
  > He now says "I'm not sure that statement is true anymore."

- On a harsh truth about human nature:
  > "All of nature, all of society, all of capitalism, all of human endeavors are underpinned by physical violence."

## 2. Recurring arguments (more than three appearances indicate a genuine belief)

### Belief 1: Specific knowledge
- Appears in the tweetstorm, the Almanack, Life Formulas, articles at nav.al, and multiple podcasts.
- Evolution of the definition: 2008, "Knowing how to do something that society cannot yet easily train other people to do" → 2018, "Knowledge you cannot be trained for. If society can train you, it can train someone else and replace you."
- Most concise statements:
  > "Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your innate talents, your genuine curiosity, and your passion. It's not by going to school for whatever is the hottest job."
  > "Specific knowledge is the set of skills that feels like play to you but looks like work to others."

### Belief 2: Leverage
- Appears in the tweetstorm, the Almanack, Life Formulas, Venture Hacks, and almost every interview.
- Naval's types of leverage:
  1. **Labor** — Requires permission and is the oldest form.
  2. **Capital** — Requires permission and someone else to give you money.
  3. **Code** — Requires no permission and has zero marginal cost.
  4. **Media** — Requires no permission and has zero marginal cost.
- Core distinction:
  > "The new forms of leverage are permissionless. They don't require somebody else's permission for you to use them or succeed."
  > "Code is probably the most powerful form of permissionless leverage. All you need is a computer — you don't need anyone's permission."

### Belief 3: Compound interest
- Appears in the tweetstorm, Life Formulas, the Almanack, and the Knowledge Project podcast.
- Naval generalizes compound interest from finance to all areas of life:
  > "All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest."

### Belief 4: Happiness is a skill
- Appears in the Almanack, the Tim Ferriss podcast (#97, #136, #473), the Knowledge Project, and Modern Wisdom.
- Core definition:
  > "Happiness is the absence of desire, especially the absence of desire for external things."
  > "Desire is a contract that you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want."
  > "Peace is happiness at rest, and happiness is peace in motion."

### Belief 5: Reading as a meta-skill
- Appears in Life Formulas, the Almanack, the Knowledge Project, the Tim Ferriss podcast, and nav.al.
- Key point:
  > "Read what you love until you love to read."
  > "Reading is the ultimate meta-skill and can be traded for anything else."
- Method: Read one to two hours a day. Read books as you read blogs, skipping around without guilt about not finishing. Pay particular attention to primary texts in foundational disciplines.

### Belief 6: Radical honesty
- Appears in the Knowledge Project podcast, the Almanack, and several interviews.
  > "I want to be able to just be me. I never want to be in an environment or around people where I have to watch what I say."

### Belief 7: Escape competition through authenticity
- Appears in the tweetstorm, the Almanack, and multiple podcasts.
  > "When you're competing with people, it's because you're copying them. Every human is different. You can escape competition through authenticity, when you realize that no one can compete with you on being you."

### Belief 8: Judgment matters more than hard work
- Appears in the tweetstorm, the Almanack, Life Formulas, and multiple podcasts.
  > "Getting rich is about knowing what to do, who to do it with, and when to do it."
  > "Wisdom is understanding the long-term consequences of your actions."
  > "Being successful is just about not making mistakes."

## 5. Timeline of intellectual development

### 2007–2010: Entrepreneur and practitioner
- The Venture Hacks blog focused on venture capital game theory and founders' rights.
- Core conviction: Information asymmetry is unfair and should be eliminated.
- Product development: From blog to AngelList.

### 2008: Life Formulas
- The first systematic presentation of his framework for life.
- Early versions of specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability already appear.
- At this stage, the presentation resembled personal notes and lacked a fully developed theory.

### 2015–2016: Philosophical turn
- Tim Ferriss podcast #97 (2015): "The Evolutionary Angel."
- Tim Ferriss podcast #136 (2016): "Happiness Hacks and 5 Chimps Theory."
- He began discussing happiness, meditation, and Eastern philosophy in public.
- This marked his shift from startup investor to angel philosopher.

### 2018: The tweetstorm breaks out
- The 39 tweets systematized his thinking about wealth.
- They compressed ten years of thought into memorable statements that people could share.
- Core innovation: Unifying specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability in one framework.

### 2020: Publication of the Almanack
- Eric Jorgenson compiled it with Naval's authorization.
- The two sections, wealth and happiness, formed a complete system.
- The free publication became an authoritative compilation of Naval's ideas.

### 2024–2025: Revision and further development
- Network State Conference (2024): Discussion of wealth, knowledge, and AI.
- "44 Harsh Truths" (March 2025): Public revision of early views.
  - He is no longer sure that happiness equals satisfaction.
  - He emphasizes dependence on context more strongly.
  - His outlook on human nature becomes harsher and more realistic.
- The Sovereign Child (2025): Applies philosophy to parenting.
- His cryptocurrency views become clearer. Bitcoin is a store of value, and crypto is the currency of AI.

### Summary of the trends
1. From **practice** to **theory**: Early practical work such as AngelList was later synthesized into frameworks.
2. From **external** to **internal**: Wealth creation → a philosophy of happiness → the search for meaning → parenting and human nature.
3. From **certainty** to **uncertainty**: Early aphorisms expressed certainty. Later remarks include more revisions and expressions of doubt.
4. **A constant thread**: The pursuit of financial freedom, freedom of thought, freedom of expression, and children's freedom.

## 6. Other key themes

### Honesty and anger
> "Anger is a hot coal you hold in your hand while waiting to throw it at somebody." (Buddhist saying Naval quotes)

> "I don't believe in anger anymore. I don't want to be angry, and I don't want to be around angry people, so I cut them out of my life."

### The meaning of life
> "There is no meaning to life. You've been dead for the history of the Universe, 10 billion years or more, and you will be dead for the next 70 billion years, until the heat death of the Universe."

> "If there were to be an answer to life's meaning, we would not be free — thus we are lucky that there is no answer."

### Foundational disciplines
> "There is no skill called 'business.' Avoid business magazines and business classes. Study microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, mathematics, and computers."

### Meditation
> "Watch your mind all day long... You can meditate 24/7. Meditation is not a sit down, close your eyes activity. Meditation is just basically watching your own thoughts like you would watch anything else in the outside world."

### Cryptocurrency and AI
> "Crypto is tech to replace socialist money with a free-market system." (November 2025)

> Naval called cryptocurrency “the currency of AI” (August 2024).

**Continuing versus revised beliefs:**
- Continuing: Specific knowledge, leverage, compound interest, reading, and honesty. These have never wavered.
- Revised: Happiness equals satisfaction while success equals dissatisfaction. In 2025 he said publicly he was not sure this was still correct.
- Developing: His view of human nature is becoming more realistic and harsher (44 Harsh Truths).
