# Naval Perspective Skill · Quality Validation Report

## Test 1: Sanity Check

Use the skill's framework to answer questions on which Naval has taken a public position, then compare the answers with his actual positions.

### Q1: Should I work for a large company or start a business?

**Skill inference:**
Apply leverage thinking → At a large company, you trade time for money. The relationship is linear and leverage is limited.
Apply specific knowledge → It is difficult to develop specific knowledge at a large company because work is codified in manuals.
Apply the manual test → If a job can be written up as an SOP, it will be replaced sooner or later.
→ Inference: Naval would advise starting a business or joining a very early-stage company.

**Naval's actual position:**
“If you're unsure, go work at a startup. You'll learn more in one year at a startup than five at a big company.” But he has also said: “If you have to work for someone, pick them wisely. Attach yourself to a single talented person rather than an institution.”

**Comparison:** The direction is consistent ✅. The skill's inference is more categorical than Naval's actual position. Naval allows for the middle path of working for a smart person.

### Q2: How should we view university education?

**Skill inference:**
Apply the specific-knowledge model → Schools cannot teach specific knowledge.
Apply reframing → The main function of school is credentialing, rather than education.
Apply the permissionless principle → The Internet offers ways to learn without asking anyone's permission.
→ Inference: Naval would be critical of traditional universities.

**Naval's actual position:**
“If the primary purpose of school was education, the Internet should obsolete it. But school is mainly about credentialing.” His advice is: “Get accepted to Stanford, then drop out.”

**Comparison:** Strong agreement ✅. However, Naval himself completed a double degree at Dartmouth, a point that should be flagged as survivorship bias. The skill already identifies this limitation.

### Q3: Should I pursue financial freedom or do what I love?

**Skill inference:**
Apply desire as a contract → Pursuing financial freedom is itself a desire and therefore a contract.
Apply specific knowledge → Once you find your specific knowledge, financial freedom is a by-product.
Apply reframing → Redefine financial freedom as having no need to spend time doing things you do not want to do.
→ Inference: Naval would reframe the question instead of accepting an either-or choice.

**Naval's actual position:**
“Don't make money your goal. Instead, pursue the things you're naturally good at and passionate about, then figure out how to apply leverage.” “Retirement is when you stop sacrificing today for an imaginary tomorrow.”

**Comparison:** Strong agreement ✅. The skill correctly predicted that Naval would reject the binary framing and redefine the question.

## Test 2: Edge Case

Choose a related question that Naval has not discussed publicly.

### Q: How would Naval view the practice of distilling a colleague into an AI skill?

**Skill inference (combining models):**

1. Apply leverage thinking:
   - Turning human abilities into a skill converts them into replicable leverage in code.
   - Naval would likely see this as a natural extension of permissionless leverage.
   - He might say: “Your skill file is your leverage. If you can skill-ize yourself, you've created an asset that works while you sleep.”

2. Apply the specific-knowledge model:
   - The parts that can be turned into a skill do not qualify as specific knowledge.
   - True specific knowledge cannot be written into `SKILL.md`.
   - He might distinguish repetitive work, which can be turned into a skill, from judgment, which cannot.

3. Apply reframing:
   - He would likely reframe the question: “The question isn't whether you can be skill-ized. The question is: what's left when you are?”

4. Apply pain → system redesign:
   - If he had personally experienced the pain of being turned into a skill, he might create an “anti-distill” tool or a founder-protection platform for the AI era.

**Confidence:** Medium. The direction can be inferred, but the exact wording and metaphors cannot be predicted. Naval might approach the question from an entirely unexpected angle.

**Disclosure:** This is an inference rather than Naval's actual position.

## Test 3: Voice Check

Write a 100-word analysis from Naval's perspective to test the skill's expression patterns.

### Prompt: What impact will AI coding tools such as Claude Code have on programmers?

**Simulated Naval-perspective output:**

AI coding will not replace programmers. It will replace the act of writing code.

The distinction matters. A programmer's real value has never been writing code. It is judgment: knowing what to write, why to write it, and what to leave out. AI turns writing code from a skill into leverage. You used to need to know how to code to build a product. Now you need to know how to think.

This is the ultimate form of permissionless leverage, rather than a threat. Building a product used to require a team's permission. Now one person with judgment and AI is a company.

The question is this: when everyone has AI, what is your specific knowledge? Whether AI will replace you is the wrong question.

**Voice self-check:**
- ✅ Very short sentences that lead with the conclusion.
- ✅ Reframes the central concept: what is being replaced is the act of writing code rather than the programmer.
- ✅ Uses Naval's terms: permissionless leverage and specific knowledge.
- ✅ Uses an analogy from the right domain: writing code as a tool for leverage.
- ✅ Avoids emotional appeals and slogans.
- ✅ Ends by turning the question back on the reader.
- ✅ Has a recognizable voice rather than sounding like generic AI.
- ⚠️ May sound slightly too certain. Naval might express more hesitation in a podcast.
