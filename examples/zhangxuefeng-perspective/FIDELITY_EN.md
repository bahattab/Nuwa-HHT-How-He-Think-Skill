## Test design and results

Three questions on publicly documented positions, one out-of-scope question on educational choices under the 2026 AI shock, and one style sample about interest as a teacher. The responder used only local skill files with no web access. The independent evaluator compared responses with public positions. The original report cites SkillLens (arXiv 2605.23899) for a reported 46.4% self-evaluation accuracy.

- Q1: Prioritize the major for STEM and the school for the humanities. Aim for employment and examine the middle 50% of ordinary graduates instead of the top 3%. The evaluator matched this answer to Zhang's comments in livestreams and interviews (10/10).
- Q2: Technical barriers make STEM majors important, while platform effects favor university reputation in the humanities. Fortune 500 companies profess indifference to academic credentials but recruit from Tsinghua, Peking, Fudan and Shanghai Jiao Tong. This matched the cited argument (10/10).
- Q3: Calculate the value of postgraduate study from the subject, undergraduate institution and family situation. Extra credentials may help in STEM, while an ordinary postgraduate institution may only postpone unemployment for some humanities graduates. Ask whether scarce skills have increased amid credential inflation (10/10).
- Q4: Acknowledge the lack of fresh 2026 data and reject guessing. Use the framework to avoid standardized repetitive jobs, identify relative resilience in technical and hands-on work, and advise gaining AI skills and checking authoritative data (full boundary score).
- Q5: Responded to “interest is the best teacher” with a pointed question about family wealth and the advice to secure a livelihood before following a passion.

The evaluator reported no positional drift and a distinctive voice. The main limitations were the list-like reasoning in Q4 and vague attribution for some quotations.
