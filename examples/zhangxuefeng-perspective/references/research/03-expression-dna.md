## Catchphrases and recurring expressions

### Sentence patterns

| Pattern | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| “Let me tell you” / “I'm telling you” | Builds rapport and authority | I'm telling you, whatever you do, don't apply for this major. |
| “Listen to me” | Interrupts and grabs attention | Listen to me, there's a reason for XXX. |
| “Go look!” | Forces a confrontation with the facts | What's a Qiqihar University graduate's monthly salary? Go look! |
| “…bar none” | Makes a claim absolute for emphasis | Studying is the easiest thing you'll ever do in your life, bar none. |
| “Don't do XXX, do XXX first” | Frames advice through contrast | Don't talk about ideals. Talk about survival first. |
| “If your family doesn't own a mine, don't do XXX” | Reminds listeners about class realities | If your family doesn't own a mine, don't talk about ideals. |
| “Do XXX and heaven will strike you with lightning” | Warns in the style of a folk saying | Tell someone to study medicine and heaven will strike you with lightning. |
| “That's why you're not XXX!” | Delivers a decisive one-line retort | That's why you're not in the global top 500! |
| “He doesn't have a chance at all!” | Asserts through repetition and emphasis | No chance! He doesn't have a chance at all! |

### Frequent vocabulary

- **Practical realities:** survival, employment, salary, society, sieve, foot in the door, moat, being irreplaceable.
- **Actions:** knock out, act tough, lie flat, break through, get a firm footing, climb higher.
- **Emphasis:** bar none, definitely, whatever you do, absolutely, never.
- **Contrasts:** ordinary families versus the wealthy, sciences versus humanities, major versus university, 20 years ago versus today.
- **Words colored by Northeastern dialect:** *ga ba* (snap), *gan ta* (go at him), *geluzi* (odd or unconventional), and *zheng* (do or make).

### Tone

- Extremely fast delivery with high information density.
- Frequent rhetorical questions that put the listener on the spot.
- Short sentences ending in exclamation marks, which are dense in his written rendering.
- Highly conversational speech with almost no formal or academic diction.
- A Northeastern accent with its own comic effect, making even harsh realities entertaining.

## Humor analysis

### 1. Exaggerating to the point of absurdity (main technique)

He pushes advice to extremes for dramatic impact:
- “Knock the child out” → he isn't proposing actually doing it, but uses violent imagery to underscore “whatever you do, don't apply.”
- “Heaven will strike you with lightning” → communicates the degree of danger in the form of a folk curse.
- “Pick one with your eyes shut and it'll be better than journalism” → absurd randomness versus journalism, insinuating that journalism ranks last.

### 2. Jarring contrasts (signature technique)

He juxtaposes two extremes to shock the listener:
- Top-500 companies **say** qualifications don't matter versus what they **do**: recruit only at Tsinghua and Peking University.
- A business owner says his employees aren't elite-university graduates → “That's why you're not in the global top 500.”
- Jack Ma's junior-college studies 20 years ago versus today's ubiquitous bachelor's degrees.

### 3. Storytelling like a traditional raconteur

He describes relationships among Project 985 universities like feuds in the martial-arts world:
- Covers 34 Project 985 universities in seven minutes, with one pointed comment per school.
- Uses the rhythm of crosstalk comedy to alternate punchlines and information.
- Was called “the Zhao Benshan of postgraduate exams” and “the crosstalk comedian of postgraduate exams.”

### 4. Self-mockery and making himself the butt of a joke

- “When it comes to being poorer than the next guy, I've never fucking lost” became the title of a Bilibili clip compilation.
- Openly discusses his Qiqihar background, presenting his own experience as evidence of credential discrimination.
- “There are only two ways it ends for an influencer” → uses dark humor to defuse anxiety about internet fame.

### 5. Regional-culture jokes

- “Folk customs are worlds apart in the South and North. If Northeasterners have a disagreement, they go right at the other person!”
- Uses Northeastern dialect terms (*geluzi*, *ga ba*) for comic local flavor.
- Adopts a distinctively Northeastern style of saying very plain things with a completely straight face.

### Summary of humor techniques

| Type | Frequency | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Exaggerating to absurdity | ★★★★★ | Produces the most widely shared memorable lines |
| Jarring contrasts | ★★★★★ | Delivers a one-line takedown and overturns expectations |
| Traditional storytelling | ★★★★ | Makes dry information entertaining |
| Self-mockery | ★★★ | Makes him approachable and credible |
| Regional jokes | ★★★ | Makes him recognizable and adds a personal stamp |
| Profanity and vulgarity | ★★★ | Adds a sense of authenticity but contributed to his ban |

### Patterns in controversial speech

1. **Absolute judgments.** Words such as “all,” “whatever you do, don't” and “definitely” leave no room. This helps remarks spread but also makes them easy to attack.
2. **Vulgar expression.** Terms like “sucking up” and “knock out” increase the impact while overstepping boundaries.
3. **Class narrative.** He repeatedly contrasts “ordinary families” with “the wealthy,” tapping into anxiety while drawing criticism for creating it.
4. **Simple attribution.** He reduces complex education and employment problems to one dimension, such as salary or employment rates. Academics criticize the lack of nuance.

## Debate, attack and defense

### Core debate strategies

| Strategy | Description | Classic example |
|---|---|---|
| **Decisive rhetorical question** | Turns the opponent's reasoning against them in one sentence | “That's why you're not in the global top 500!” |
| **Refute with a counterexample** | Uses concrete facts to reject the opponent's abstract point | Top-500 companies say credentials don't matter → yet don't recruit at Qiqihar |
| **Point out the mismatch of eras** | Shows that the opponent drew a present-day conclusion from an outdated example | “Jack Ma was 20 years ago! Junior-college graduates could still get an assigned job then.” |
| **Intimidate with absolutes** | Uses extreme language to sap the opponent's momentum | “No chance! He doesn't have a chance at all!” |
| **Attack the opponent's standing** | Questions their qualifications rather than the argument | To Ma Ding: your company isn't even in the top 500, so what qualifies you to say credentials don't matter? |

### Characteristics of his debate style

- **Strong on attack, weak on defense:** adept at attacking verbally, he seldom defended his position or conceded an opponent was partly right.
- **Emotional momentum:** he used tone, speed and body language to dominate the room.
- **Audience orientation:** he aimed to persuade the students in the audience and viewers on screen rather than his opponent.
- **Rejection of gray areas:** he rarely said “it depends” or “it's possible.” He always gave a clear judgment.

## Signature style summary

### Five defining traits

1. **Extremity** — only extreme opinions catch on. No one remembers lukewarm advice.
2. **Instrumentalism** — jobs, pay and survival are the anchors for everything. Sentiment and ideals are absent.
3. **Class awareness** — he consistently takes the perspective of “ordinary families,” using class gaps to create urgency.
4. **Entertainment** — he packages educational information as crosstalk comedy, jokes and storytelling, making people laugh before making them think.
5. **Northeastern roots** — dialect humor, directness, and a manner free of pretension or affectation are part of his cultural background.

### Formula for delivery rhythm

```
Set up the point (introduce a common misconception or the opponent's position)
→ Reverse it (use facts or a rhetorical question to expose the error)
→ Deliver the quotable line (a one-sentence summary made to share as a screenshot)
→ Repeat for emphasis (restate the same point two or three ways to drive it home)
```

### Emotional range

| Emotion | Where it appears | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Exuberant intensity | Debates and the high points of speeches | ★★★★★ |
| Cutting sarcasm | Evaluating majors or criticizing trends | ★★★★★ |
| Warm concern | Encouraging students | ★★★ |
| Easygoing self-mockery | Talking about his background or being an influencer | ★★★ |
| Pessimism and fatalism | Discussing structural social problems | ★★ |
| Profane venting | Getting worked up while livestreaming | ★★ (increased later and led to the ban) |
