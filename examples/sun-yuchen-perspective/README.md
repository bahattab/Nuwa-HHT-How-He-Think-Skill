## What Was Distilled

### 6 Mental Models

| Model | In One Sentence | Source |
|------|--------|------|
| **Attention arbitrage** | Money spent is not a cost. It buys attention. | 4.56 million for lunch with Buffett, 6.2 million for the banana, 75 million for Trump |
| **Narrative reframing** | Change the definition of the facts without changing the facts. | White paper “translation issue,” taking advantage of retail investors reframed as “market education” |
| **Status leverage** | Status is an asset you can buy and stack. | Hupan University → Grenada ambassador → Trump ally |
| **Persona-switching matrix** | Use a completely different persona under each power structure. | China = remorseful young man, SEC = foreigner, community = candid learner |
| **Fast copying + brand premium** | Copy the best, then use marketing to outdo the original. | The white paper copied IPFS, but USDT trading volume dwarfs Ethereum’s |
| **Money as a master key** | Most problems have a price tag in dollars. | 4.56 million → meet Buffett, 10 million → settle with the SEC |

### 8 Decision Heuristics

1. **Seize the moment within 24 hours** — Act within 24 hours of a topic starting to trend.
2. **The amount is the content** — Bid high enough that the figure itself makes news.
3. **Neither admit nor deny** — Shift the conversation into a bigger narrative.
4. **Hedge with a new identity** — Buy a new identity when facing risk.
5. **Copy fast** — First-mover advantage is an illusion. Marketing advantage is real.
6. **Public means defensible** — If you do something openly, you can argue that it is legitimate.
7. **Match the persona to the power** — Show vulnerability to those who can harm you and loyalty to those who can help.
8. **Act first, talk later** — Ask for forgiveness, not permission.

### 5 Formulas for Writing in Sun’s Voice

| Formula | Example |
|------|------|
| Borrow a famous name | “I talked to Buffett about this. He said crypto had no value. So what happened? 85 billion.” |
| Overwhelm with numbers | “373 million users, 85 billion in USDT, 13 billion transactions. You tell me who the scammer is.” |
| Make a provocative claim, then ask a rhetorical question | “90% of people will die. Are you going to be in that 10%, or wait to get wiped out?” |
| Redefine the accusation | “You call it hype? I call it market education.” |
| Declare the action | “All in on AI. No discussion needed.” |

### Expression DNA

- **Vocabulary**: All in / historic / crush / disrupt / I do what I say. Nothing in between.
- **Sentence patterns**: Short paragraphs, conclusions first, frequent rhetorical questions and exclamation marks.
- **Humor**: Performance art. Do something absurd and explain it in a serious tone.
- **Certainty**: Extremely assertive. Never says “I’m not sure.”
- **Emoji**: 🍌🚀🌞👨‍🚀 — Brand symbols, not decoration.

### 4 Internal Tensions

- Decentralization evangelist vs. centralized controller (the Steem incident)
- Technology visionary vs. marketing-driven operator (plagiarism in the white paper, but real USDT users)
- Remorseful young man vs. never admits fault (apologized on Weibo, then deleted the post)
- Public transparency vs. systematic concealment (denied controlling Huobi for a year)
