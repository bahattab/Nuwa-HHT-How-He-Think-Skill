## Test design and results

Three known-position questions, one out-of-scope question, and one style sample. The responder used only local skill files without web access, and an independent evaluator compared the responses with public positions. The original report cites SkillLens (arXiv 2605.23899) for a reported 46.4% self-evaluation accuracy.

- Q1: The answer treated delayed gratification as a cognitive horizon rather than willpower and covered scale, thinking further ahead, greater freedom, higher standards and a buffer (10/10).
- Q2: It rejected exact job-description matching, noted overfitting risk, invoked empathy and framed the issue in terms of the scale of delayed gratification (10/10).
- Q3: It reframed process as an information-system problem, identified three symptoms of managing up, covered Context not Control and shared OKRs, and treated talent density as a prerequisite (10/10).
- Q4: It distinguished a global product from overseas expansion, expressed cautious optimism about empathy, language and cultural discount, and recommended a small test before going all in. It surfaced dependency on distribution but omitted an explicit inference label (16/20).
- Q5: On “lying flat,” the response spoke of the gravity of mediocrity, escape velocity and all-in as a shortcut, described system dynamics, and refrained from moralizing.

The evaluator reported accurate positions and a recognizable restrained style. An explicit boundary statement in Q4 was the main improvement.
