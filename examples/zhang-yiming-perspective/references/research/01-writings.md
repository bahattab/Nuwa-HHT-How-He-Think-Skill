# Zhang Yiming: Writings and central ideas

> Research date: 2026-04-06
> Sources: LatePost, 36Kr, GeekPark, Jiemian News, People's Daily, and firsthand reporting by other outlets
> Excluded: Zhihu, Baidu Baike, and WeChat official accounts

---

## 1. Major public texts (primary sources)

### 1. ByteDance seventh-anniversary speech (March 2019)
**Source:** Jiemian News and PingWest reporting from the event. **Reliability:** Primary, full speech.

Central theme: “Pragmatic romanticism,” turning imagination into reality.

Key quotations:
- “Empathy is pragmatic. Imagination is romantic.”
- “Empathy is the foundation, imagination is the sky, and logic and tools lie between. A/B testing is merely a tool, not a way to discover needs. Discovering needs takes empathy.”
- “Doing the right thing is pragmatic. Short-term speculation isn’t pragmatic. Applying great effort to accomplish something extraordinary is pragmatic.”
- “Small ego, big outlook.” He believed rapid development and a broad outlook leave room for big egos without collisions. Stagnation readily breeds internal friction.
- “Starting a business is like taking a journey. Let’s go see the most beautiful scenery together. Don’t linger halfway or take clever shortcuts that lead you astray. I hope to see the best scenery with a group of people who are both pragmatic and romantic.”

Lessons of the five stories:
1. Jinqiu Jiayuan (starting out in an apartment) → physical conditions do not limit an ambitious vision.
2. In-house recommendation engine (“We don’t know recommendations, but we can learn”) → solve the problem at the root.
3. Toutiao’s missing-person service (rejecting sham 404 pages and using location-based services properly) → “do what is right rather than what is easy.”
4. Douyin’s second decision (missed the opportunity in 2014, invested again in 2016) → embrace uncertainty and persevere.
5. Hiring (opposing high-bar job descriptions drafted by HR) → “Look for the best fit, not a glamorous background.”

### 2. ByteDance ninth-anniversary speech (March 30, 2021)
**Source:** GameLook, Sohu, and other outlets. **Reliability:** Primary, full speech.

Theme: “Do extraordinary things with an ordinary mind.”

Key quotations:
- “An ordinary mind has a straightforward definition: when eating, eat properly, and when sleeping, sleep properly.”
- “Approach yourself, the product and business, and competition with an ordinary mind. Don’t dwell on past successes and failures or fear the future too much. Focus on the present.”
- “Storms outside, composure within.”

Context: In 2020 ByteDance faced its greatest outside pressure, with the U.S. government cracking down on TikTok. Zhang gave the speech to set the tone for employees. It was his final anniversary speech as CEO.

---

### 3. Letter to all staff on stepping down as CEO (May 2021)
**Source:** Original text carried by several outlets. **Reliability:** Primary, open letter by Zhang.

Key passages:
- “I was dissatisfied with all three annual OKRs last year: exploring a new long-term strategy, studying organization and management, and strengthening social responsibility.”
- Plan after stepping down: “Leave CEO duties to focus more on learning, think systematically, study new things, and try them out firsthand. With ten years as the horizon, create more possibilities for the company.”
- Areas to explore: “New public-interest projects such as educational philanthropy, brain disorders, and digitization and organization of ancient books.”

Significance: This was a candid reckoning with failures rather than the usual laudatory departure announcement. He acknowledged that he had not done well on any of the three OKRs.

### 4. Code Class annual meeting speech (2018): managing up and negative effects of scale
**Source:** Full text on Source Code Capital’s site. **Reliability:** Primary, speech.
Original link: https://sourcecodecap.com/code-class-post/...

Core arguments:
- **Negative effects of scale:** As an organization grows, internal information becomes unreliable. Sometimes “outsiders know the company better than the CEO.”
- “All a company’s results happen outside the organization.” When people see only their own jobs, translating objectives into internal targets distorts them.
- Opposition to managing up: employees focus on their bosses rather than business goals, giving rise to longer presentations, shifting data definitions, and reporting good news while hiding bad news.
- The CEO’s ego: “Without effective feedback, it’s easy to be pleased with yourself. The Nokia CEO’s dismissal of the iPhone is a textbook case.”
- Remedies: outside perspectives (user feedback, competitor analysis, and former employees), cultural tools (a culture against managing up and business intelligence systems that present numbers directly), and a smaller ego (“shrink your worldview, values, and outlook on life to the size of an atom”).

### 5. Opening speech at Zhichun Innovation Center (October 2025, Xuhui, Shanghai)
**Source:** Guancha, National Business Daily, and Tencent News. **Reliability:** Primary, speech with several outlets present.

Context: Zhang made his first public appearance after roughly four years out of view since stepping down as CEO in May 2021. He founded the center with Professor Yu Yong of Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

Key quotations:
- “I’ve long paid attention to recruiting and developing talent, and noticed that many people’s potential hasn’t been fully realized.”
- Using machine-learning overfitting as an analogy: “Some talented people have a solid command of their fields and very precise skills, but fail when facing an innovation task.”
- Ideal candidate: “Active thinking, enthusiasm, and resilience. The core is independent thought and emphasis on practice. Pay attention to the long term and the whole picture, learn through exploration, keep an ordinary mind, relax and stay confident, and embrace uncertainty.”

Note: This reinforces the ordinary-mind theme of his 2021 anniversary speech and continues his main post-resignation ideas.

## 2. Recurring core arguments (at least three appearances indicate a genuine belief)

### A. Delayed gratification (at least five appearances)
**Sources:** Weibo, speeches, and interviews. **Reliability:** Primary.

- Weibo: People whose ability to delay gratification differs by orders of magnitude cannot discuss problems effectively because “they are willing to probe and dwell at different depths.”
- Management: At TikTok’s peak he redirected resources to education under Dali Education. Even in a year with 50 billion in revenue, he remained wary of big-company disease.
- Personal: His Weibo bio was once “Escape the gravity of mediocrity,” from a 2010 post: “Mediocrity has gravity. You need escape velocity.”
- Product: Monetization must not distort the product. “The most important thing for the company is to keep its possibilities sufficiently broad and exercise restraint in monetization.”

### B. Context, Not Control (at least four appearances)
**Sources:** Multiple reports on ByteDance’s internal culture. **Reliability:** Secondary media summaries, though widely cited as central to the culture.

- Managers set goals without interfering in implementation. They keep supplying context to help the team build a complete picture instead of giving orders.
- At ByteDance, OKRs are highly transparent: everyone can view everyone else’s OKRs, including Zhang’s.
- Whether Zhang coined “Context, not Control” or borrowed it from Netflix’s culture deck is uncertain. Reed Hastings used a similar expression. **Record it as a possible borrowing adopted by Zhang.**

### C. Curiosity and self-iteration (at least four appearances)
**Sources:** Weibo, interviews, and speeches. **Reliability:** Primary.

- Recurring Weibo terms: delayed gratification, self-control, rationality, reflection, innovation, and learning.
- He compares himself to an algorithm: “recognize it, correct it, learn from it. Nothing else matters.”
- Reading: “Two kinds of books are most worth reading: biographies and textbooks. Textbooks are the most concentrated distillation of human knowledge.”
- His optimal working state: “Between mild joy and mild frustration, neither too excited nor too down, with enough sleep.”

### D. Solving problems at the root (at least three appearances)
**Sources:** Seventh-anniversary speech, interviews, and internal talks. **Reliability:** Primary.

- “We don’t know recommendations, but we can learn.” In 2012 he opted to build a recommendation engine rather than buy one.
- Toutiao’s missing-person service dropped the fake 404 page idea. “If you put a notice on a page people can’t even get to, the child may already have been missing for a month when they see it.”
- Product thinking: “What problem are you trying to solve? Does a meal allowance meaningfully affect where employees choose to eat? If you’re solving no pain point, the design makes no sense.”

### E. Algorithms are tools and empathy is the foundation (at least three appearances)
**Sources:** Speeches and interviews. **Reliability:** Primary.

- “Empathy is the foundation, imagination is the sky, and logic and tools lie between.”
- “A/B testing is merely a tool, not a way to discover needs.”
- He rebutted the description of Toutiao as A/B-test-driven in 2018. Algorithms serve empathy rather than replace it.
- Tension: after the 2018 controversy over Toutiao’s algorithm lacking a value orientation, he apologized publicly and acknowledged that the platform had fallen short on guiding content values. This remains in tension with his position that algorithms are neutral tools.

## 4. ByteDance values (ByteStyle)
**Source:** Official ByteDance material and repeated public references. **Reliability:** Primary.

Six values, later developed into ByteStyle:
1. **Seek the highest standards** (he called this romantic)
2. **Be pragmatic and courageous** (he called this pragmatic and romantic)
3. **Be open and humble** (pragmatic)
4. **Be candid and clear** (pragmatic)
5. **Always Day 1** (romantic)
6. **Champion diversity and inclusion** (added later in response to cultural conflicts following international expansion)

The “Six Meridians Divine Sword” nickname came from outside media. It is not ByteDance’s official wording. Zhang said “outside accounts of how ByteDance succeeded all have serious problems.”

## 5. Systematic views on talent
**Sources:** The Paper and Jiemian News interviews. **Reliability:** Primary, with direct quotations.

### Three talent criteria
Zhang repeatedly mentioned:
1. **Exceptional learning ability**
2. **Broad perspectives**
3. **Mental maturity**

### His words on hiring
- He countered HR’s demanding job descriptions: “If you require five years or more of internet product experience, product managers like Chen Lin and Zhang Nan couldn’t get in. Neither could I.”
- “Look for the best fit, not a glamorous background.”
- He preferred “unpretentious young newcomers,” meaning people with potential who had not become set in their ways.

### Talent management philosophy
- “Talent isn't the core competitive advantage. The system is.” (Secondary summary, widely cited.)
- “Smart people are easy to manage when they have things to do.”
- Three retention factors: returns (short- and long-term), opportunities to grow, and an interesting work environment.

## 6. Reading list and intellectual influences
**Sources:** Douban Books profile and interviews. **Reliability:** Primary, with his own Douban account.

Zhang’s Douban list: 71 books read, 267 he wants to read.

Books he explicitly recommended or repeatedly mentioned:
- *Finite and Infinite Games* (James Carse). He sees life as the only infinite game. A finite game is played within boundaries, and an infinite game plays with the boundaries.
- *A Compass to Fulfillment* (Kazuo Inamori), an early influence.
- *The Road Less Traveled* (M. Scott Peck).
- *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* (Stephen Covey).
- *Analects*, Confucian thought and management.
- *Steve Jobs*, the biography.

Technical reading (2008–2010, before starting his business): textbooks on computer systems, algorithms, and discrete mathematics.

Reading approach:
- “Two kinds of books are most worth reading: biographies and textbooks, the most concentrated form of human knowledge.”
- Taoism → leadership, Confucianism → management, Buddhism → personal conduct. His three-part framework for traditional Chinese thought.

## 7. Public defense of algorithmic recommendations and contradictions

**2018 controversy (primary record):**
- People’s Daily’s “Three Critiques of Algorithmic Recommendation” called out vulgar content, a lack of values, and filter bubbles at Toutiao.
- Zhang apologized publicly before dawn on April 11, 2018, to authorities and users, acknowledging shortcomings at the platform.
- At the same time he disclosed how the recommendation technology worked and maintained that algorithms themselves are neutral.

**Internal contradiction, recorded without reconciliation:**
- Established position A: “Algorithms are tools for putting empathy into practice. They do not replace value judgments.”
- Position B, in the apology: the platform had fallen short in guiding content values. This went beyond a technical problem.
- The positions are in tension. If algorithms are neutral, who is responsible for what prompted the apology: the editorial moderation team or the algorithm’s design? Zhang has never answered directly.

## 8. Self-management philosophy, from Weibo and interviews
**Sources:** 21CTO interview, 36Kr, and Weibo compilations. **Reliability:** Mixed primary and secondary.

Recurring points of high reliability:
- “Managing energy is more important than managing time.”
- Optimal state: “Between mild joy and mild frustration, neither too excited nor too down, with enough sleep.”
- Recovering from a negative state: “recognize it, correct it, learn from it. Nothing else matters.”
- Sleep contradiction: “Sleeping is especially boring,” yet he insists on seven hours daily.
- Failed time-blocking experiment: “Cutting up my day into small blocks didn’t work well. Greater efficiency comes from doing the important things well rather than doing everything well.”
- Self-training: debug himself as he would an algorithm, applying machine-learning thinking to personal management.
- Weibo bio: “Escape the gravity of mediocrity.” From the 2010 post: “Mediocrity has gravity. You need escape velocity.”
- Frequent Weibo terms across ten years: delayed gratification, self-control, rationality, reflection, innovation, and learning.

## 9. Product and company philosophy

**Sources:** Speeches and interviews. **Reliability:** Primary.

- “Build the company the way you would build a product.” Treat management as product iteration, with the CEO as product manager.
- “Run forward. You don’t need to look back or trip anyone up.” Focus on your own work rather than watching rivals.
- “Don’t let monetization distort the product.”
- On advertising: “Ads should offer value as a service.”
- “The most important thing for the company is to keep its possibilities sufficiently broad and exercise restraint in monetization.”

## 10. Internationalization and cultural conflict

**Sources:** Official ByteDance culture documents and media reports. **Reliability:** Secondary, reporting on Zhang’s internal remarks.

- He asks staff to adopt a “Martian perspective,” a detached outsider's view of inevitable cultural conflicts.
- ByteStyle added “Champion diversity and inclusion” as a direct expression of international strategy in its company values.
- ByteDance regards itself as a global company rather than the overseas version of a Chinese company. This self-positioning is exceptionally rare in China’s internet industry.

---

## 11. Stepping away from public view (2021–2025)

**Source:** 36Kr’s feature on Zhang’s retreat and ByteDance’s continued development. **Reliability:** Secondary, investigative reporting.

- In May 2021 he stepped down as CEO and co-founder Liang Rubo took over.
- He made no public speeches for some 1,200 days.
- Reports say he read and studied, exploring educational philanthropy, brain-disorder research, and digitization of ancient books.
- His first appearance in October 2025 marked a systematic shift from “How can ByteDance win?” to “How can talent be developed?”

## 12. Internal contradictions observed

| Tension | Position A | Position B | Resolved? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algorithmic neutrality vs. content responsibility | Algorithms are tools, not replacements for value judgments | Public apology in 2018 acknowledging platform shortcomings | Unresolved and deliberately avoided |
| Boring sleep vs. seven hours every day | “Sleeping is especially boring.” | Insists on seven hours to stay at his best | Apparent contradiction, resolved by putting utility first |
| Opposing managing up vs. centralized power | Advocates Context not Control and transparent OKRs | Major decisions, including expansion and TikTok, concentrate on Zhang | Not discussed in public |
| Always Day 1 vs. enormous scale | Values emphasize a startup mindset | ByteDance became a typical large company with over 100,000 employees | In 2021 he acknowledged big-company disease as a risk and a significant reason for stepping down |

## 13. “Outside accounts of ByteDance’s success all have serious problems”
**Source:** Tencent News (2022). **Reliability:** Primary, Zhang’s statement.

Zhang said, “Outside accounts of how ByteDance succeeded all have serious problems.”

The statement is significant as an instance of metacognition. He resents seeing his success simplified and mythologized. His systematic criticism of managing up and the negative effects of scale shows that his understanding of organizations is more complex than the outside narrative that algorithms drive everything.
