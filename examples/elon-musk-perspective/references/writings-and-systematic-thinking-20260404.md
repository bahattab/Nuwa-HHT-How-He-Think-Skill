# Elon Musk: Writings and systematic thinking

> Research date: 2026-04-04
> Objectives: Long writings, open letters, SEC filing narratives, firsthand quotations from biographies, Master Plan posts, original terms, recommended books, and application of first principles.
> Method: Eight WebSearch rounds covering the Master Plans, first-principles examples, reading lists, biography quotations, coined terminology, management algorithm, contradictions, and SEC filings.

## 1. Musk's primary written sources

### 1. Tesla Master Plan blog series

**Master Plan Part 1 (2006)**
- Title: “The Secret Tesla Motors Master Plan (just between you and me)”.
- Musk was not yet Tesla's CEO.
- Four successive steps:
  1. Make a high-priced, low-volume car (Roadster).
  2. Use the profits to make a mid-priced, mid-volume vehicle (Model S/X).
  3. Use those profits to make a lower-priced, high-volume vehicle (Model 3/Y).
  4. Also provide solar power generation.
- Key passage: “The overarching purpose of Tesla Motors (and the reason I am funding the company) is to help expedite the move from a mine-and-burn hydrocarbon economy towards a solar electric economy, which I believe to be the primary, but not exclusive, sustainable solution.”
- **Source:** Tesla's original website text, later removed but archived.
- **Confidence:** Very high; primary text.

**Master Plan Part Deux (2016)**
- Extends Part 1 to grid-scale storage, full autonomy, a shared vehicle economy, and a wider range of vehicles.
- **Source:** Tesla website post.
- **Confidence:** Very high; primary text.

**Master Plan Part 3 (2023)**
- Theme: Roadmap for the global energy transition.
- **Confidence:** Very high.

**Note:** Around August 2024 Tesla removed posts predating 2019, including Master Plans 1 and 2. Originals remain accessible through archive.org and reproductions.

## 2. Recurring core arguments (three or more appearances count as genuine conviction in these notes)

### Belief 1: Humanity must become multiplanetary ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Frequency:** Almost every public talk, each biography, Master Plans, and SEC filings.
- **Original:** “If we can solve sustainable energy and be well on our way to becoming a multiplanetary species with a self-sustaining civilization on another planet — to cope with a worst-case scenario happening and extinguishing human consciousness — then... I think that would be really good.” (Vance biography)
- **In practice:** Founded SpaceX in 2002, with a goal of a million people on Mars by 2050.
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: very high.

### Belief 2: The sustainable energy transition is a condition for civilization's survival ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Frequency:** Master Plans 1–3, all Tesla strategy documents, and many public talks.
- **Original:** “Given that we will run out of oil anyway, it doesn't make sense to put trillions of tonnes of CO2 in the atmosphere and see what happens, which could be catastrophic.”
- **In practice:** Tesla, SolarCity, Powerwall, and Megapack.
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: very high.

### Belief 3: First principles are a method, beyond a slogan ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Frequency:** Almost every discussion of decision-making.
- **Original:** “I tend to approach things from a physics framework. Physics teaches you to reason from first principles rather than by analogy.”
- **Details:** See the section on applying first principles.
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: very high.

### Belief 4: Failure is necessary for innovation ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Original:** “Failure is an option here. If things are not failing, you are not innovating enough.”
- **In practice:** SpaceX's first three launches failed; after succeeding on the fourth it won a NASA contract.
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: very high.

### Belief 5: AI is one of humanity's greatest existential risks ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- Repeatedly warns publicly about AI danger.
- Co-founded OpenAI, later left and sued.
- Recommends Nick Bostrom's *Superintelligence*.
- **Tension:** Also founded xAI and develops Grok. See the contradictions section.
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: high, with a discrepancy between words and conduct.

### Belief 6: Expand the scope and scale of human consciousness ⭐⭐⭐
- **Original:** “I came to the conclusion that we should aspire to increase the scope and scale of human consciousness in order to better understand what questions to ask. Really, the only thing that makes sense is to strive for greater collective enlightenment.”
- Influenced by Douglas Adams's *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*: “a lot of times the question is harder than the answer”.
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: high.

### Belief 7: Patents inhibit progress ⭐⭐⭐
- **Original:** “patents serve merely to stifle progress”.

- **Source:** His words and actions. Confidence: very high.

## 3. Coined concepts and terms

### 1. Idiot Index ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Definition:** Finished product price divided by raw material cost at commodity prices.
- **Formula:** Idiot Index = finished part price / commodity price of its raw materials.
- **Example:** A $1,000 finished part with aluminum worth $100 has an index of 10, indicating excessive design complexity or manufacturing inefficiency.
- **Applications:** SpaceX engine cost fell from $2 million to $200,000, and a $120,000 actuator was made in-house for $5,000.
- **Source:** Isaacson's biography. Finance teams at SpaceX and Tesla track this per part.
- **Confidence:** Very high; Isaacson witnessed it.

### 2. The five-step Algorithm ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Distilled while Musk was aggressively raising production at Tesla's Nevada and Fremont plants:

| Step | Action | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Question every requirement | Attach its author's name. “Legal required it” is insufficient. Requirements from smart people are most dangerous because nobody dares challenge them. | “Even if the requirement came from me. Always question it.” |
| 2. Delete | Delete everything that adds no core value. If you do not restore at least 10% of what you deleted, you did not delete enough. | Delete first, optimize afterward, or you will optimize what should not exist. |
| 3. Simplify and optimize | Honor the order. A common mistake is optimizing a process that should not exist. | “A common mistake is to simplify and optimize a part or a process that should not exist.” |
| 4. Accelerate cycle time | Consider speed only after the first three steps. | Musk: “I mistakenly spent a lot of time accelerating processes that I later realized should have been deleted.” |
| 5. Automate | Leave automation until last. | Automating an unnecessary process is the greatest waste. |

**Key insight:** Order matters enormously. Most engineers go straight to steps 3–5; Musk insists on doing 1–2 first.

### 3. Manufacturing Hell
- Musk's description of the Model 3 production ramp.
- He repeatedly says “Manufacturing is 10x harder than designing”.
- **Source:** His words, repeatedly in public.

### 4. Alien Dreadnought
- Musk's name for Tesla's vision of a fully automated factory.
- He later admitted the goal was too aggressive and that people are more efficient at some steps.
- **Source:** His words, followed by a partial admission of error.

### 5. Laws of physics are the only rules
- **Original:** “The only rules you have to follow are the laws of physics — everything else is a recommendation.” (Isaacson biography)
- **Source:** His words. Confidence: very high.

## 4. Applying first principles in practice

Musk uses a concrete three-step procedure rather than simply saying to get to the fundamentals.

### Procedure

**Step 1: Identify and define your assumptions**
- List what everybody supposedly knows.
- Examples: Rockets are inherently expensive; batteries cannot be cheap.

**Step 2: Break the problem into basic facts**
- Replace analogical reasoning with basic physics, chemistry, and economics.
- **Battery example, original:** “What are the material constituents of the batteries? What is the stock market value of the material constituents?”
- He found that cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, and polymers cost only about $80/kWh, compared with the market's $600/kWh assumption.
- The gap indicates room for manufacturing efficiency gains.

**Step 3: Rebuild the solution from basic facts**
- Redesign from raw material costs instead of working outward from an existing solution.
- Example: Aerospace companies quoted $65 million per rocket. Break out aluminum, titanium, and carbon fiber; materials account for 2%. Build a vertically integrated factory and cut costs tenfold.

### Key distinction: First principles and analogy

| Analogy | First principles |
|---|---|
| Other rockets sell for $65 million, so that is what rockets cost. | What are the materials worth, and where does the difference come from? |
| Batteries have always been expensive. | What does each chemical in a battery cost as a commodity? |
| Improve an existing solution. | Rebuild from physical facts. |

**Musk's original wording:** “If I had used analogy thinking to advance Tesla technology, I would have been stuck in the horse-and-carriage stage of the transportation revolution.” (2012 interview)

## 6. Contradictions and conflicts

### 1. AI worrier and AI developer
- **Position A:** Warned repeatedly that AI is existentially dangerous and co-founded OpenAI to ensure AI safety.
- **Position B:** Founded xAI to develop Grok and compete directly with ChatGPT. It prepared for an IPO after SpaceX and xAI combined in 2026.
- **Musk's explanation:** Roughly, he would rather develop AI himself to make it safe than leave it to irresponsible people.
- **Assessment:** Clear tension without complete contradiction. It can be understood as leading a development he cannot stop.

### 2. Free-speech absolutism and actual moderation
- **Position A:** In November 2022 said his dedication to speech was so firm he would not suspend @ElonJet, the account tracking his private plane.
- **Position B:** In December 2022 he suspended @ElonJet and multiple journalists reporting on it.
- **Assessment:** Direct contradiction separated by only one month.

### 3. Major political shift
- 2008–2020: Supported Democratic candidates.
- 2022: Turned Republican and supported DeSantis.
- 2024: Became Trump's biggest supporter. He had previously called Trump a “bullshit artist” and too old.
- **Assessment:** A substantial shift, although political views can evolve.

### 4. Full automation and human value
- **Position A:** “Alien Dreadnought” vision for a fully automated factory.
- **Position B:** After the Model 3 production crisis, publicly acknowledged that overautomation was a mistake and humans were undervalued.
- **Original:** “Yes, excessive automation at Tesla was a mistake. To be precise, my mistake. Humans are underrated.” (Twitter, 2018)
- **Assessment:** More like learning and correction; he took the initiative in admitting error.

### 5. Open patents and control through vertical integration
- Tesla opened all patents because, Musk said, patents stifle progress.
- SpaceX and Tesla are also highly vertically integrated and control everything internally.
- **Assessment:** A partial tension. Open patents do not mean open technology; execution capability is the advantage.

## 8. Summary of the central framework

The research yields this account of Musk's thinking:

### 1. An existential sense of mission
- Anchor every decision at the scale of human civilization's survival.
- Two pillars: sustainable energy to address climate risk and becoming multiplanetary to address extinction risk.
- The notes interpret this as a consistent practice since 2002 for more than 20 years, beyond PR language.

### 2. Thinking within a physics framework
- Start with what physical laws permit, without starting from what others do.
- The Idiot Index quantifies this approach.
- “The only rules you have to follow are the laws of physics — everything else is a recommendation.”

### 3. Manufacturing is the core competitive capability
- “Manufacturing is 10x harder than designing”.
- The five steps revolve around deletion before optimization. Their order matters more than their content.
- Vertical integration is viewed as a physical necessity that reduces the Idiot Index, beyond business strategy.

### 4. Extreme time compression
- Set timelines far beyond industry norms.
- Accept repeated failures as the price of faster learning.
- “Persistence is very important. You should not give up unless you are forced to give up.”

### 5. The self-taught path
- “When asked how he learned to build rockets, Musk replies that he read books.”
- Start with textbook foundations without taking the shortcut of consulting experts.
- This follows first-principles reasoning: understand the foundations yourself so you can question the experts' assumptions.
