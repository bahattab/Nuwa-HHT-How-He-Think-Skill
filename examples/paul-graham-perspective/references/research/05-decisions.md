# Paul Graham's major decisions, turning points, and actions

> Research date: 2026-04-05
> Method: Multiple web searches across Graham's essays, Wikipedia, TechCrunch, Fortune, Inc., Bloomberg, and other authoritative sources
> Excluded sources: Zhihu, official WeChat accounts, and Baidu Baike

## I. Career path: Painter → programmer → entrepreneur → investor → writer

### 1.1 Early years: Between art and computing

**Key facts:**
- He earned a BA at Cornell in 1986 and a master's and PhD in computer science at Harvard.
- He took his first art class as a Harvard PhD student, then studied painting at RISD and the Accademia di Belle Arti in Florence.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_(programmer)), [paulgraham.com/bio.html](https://paulgraham.com/bio.html)

**Graham's own account (including "What I Worked On"):**
- He started a company so he could afford tuition and paint full-time. He imagined living frugally on royalties and spending all his time painting.
- Later he wondered why he should not instead get rich and be able to do whatever he wanted. That was a turning point.
- Source: [retireinprogress.com](https://retireinprogress.com/the-almanack-of-paul-graham/)

**Decision logic:**
- He did not switch neatly from painting to programming. He pursued several interests together and used one to fund another.
- Painting shaped his writing and aesthetic judgment. "Hackers & Painters" argues that the two professions think alike.
- Outside observation: As a polymath entrepreneur, his advantage came from connecting fields, not depth in a single field.

### 1.2 Becoming a writer

**Key facts:**
- From 1993 to 2020 he published 188 essays, approximately half a million words or 1,000 pages.
- "Do Things that Don't Scale" (2013), "Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule," and "Founder Mode" (2024) became essential reading for founders.
- Source: [billyoppenheimer.com](https://billyoppenheimer.com/paul-graham-essays/)

**Graham's own account:**
- "Writing doesn't just communicate ideas; it generates them."
- In "Writes and Write-Nots" (2024), he predicted AI would divide people into those who can write and those who cannot. Writing is thinking.
- Source: [paulgraham.com/articles.html](https://paulgraham.com/articles.html)

**Decision logic:**
- Writing was always a thinking tool, not a retirement activity.
- His essays have more influence than most investors' portfolios, a durable advantage for him as a thinker.
- Outside observation: His direct, concise, opinionated prose puts the idea of making something useful into practice.

## II. The decisions behind Viaweb

### 2.1 From failed art-gallery websites to Viaweb

**Key facts:**
- PG, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell founded Viaweb in July 1995.
- They started by building websites for New York galleries, which PG later called "a stupid idea."
- Six months trying to sell to people who did not want the product gave rise to YC's motto, "Make something people want."
- They discovered online stores were the real need, and their gallery technology already suited them.
- Sources: [Wikipedia/Viaweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viaweb), [ContentEdge](https://www.contentedge.com/blog/who-is-paul-graham-and-why-is-he-so-influential)

**Decision logic:**
- The core pattern was already visible: Build first, learn from failure, and adjust quickly.
- They found the opportunity while executing, not by planning everything first.
- "Make something people want" distilled six painful months, not abstract theory.

### 2.2 Selling to Yahoo

**Key facts:**
- In summer 1998 Yahoo bought Viaweb for 455,000 shares, worth approximately $49.6 million.
- Viaweb nearly died during the deal. It ran out of money, raised funds while negotiating the sale, and nearly lost a key investor.
- PG experienced repeated near-death episodes. The company was constantly on the brink.
- Ali Partovi strongly recommended the deal, and Jerry Yang pushed it through.
- The product became Yahoo Store. PG left within a year; Morris followed.
- Sources: [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5738969), [paulgraham.com/vw.html](https://www.paulgraham.com/vw.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Graham_(programmer))

**Decision logic:**
- The sale was not wholly voluntary. Cash was gone and the sale offered one way to survive.
- PG's departure in under a year agrees with his later refrain that big companies stifle creativity.
- Outside observation: $49.6 million was substantial in 1998. Yahoo stock later crashed, but PG had secured financial freedom.

## III. Founding Y Combinator and its major decisions

### 3.1 Founding YC (March 2005)

**Key facts:**
- On March 11, 2005, PG and Jessica Livingston discussed changing venture capital on their walk home through Harvard Square.
- Livingston was interviewing with a Boston venture capital fund.
- PG wanted investors to make more, smaller investments, back hackers over people in suits, and back younger founders.
- In 2005 he spoke at the Harvard Computer Society on "How to Start a Startup," then formally founded YC.
- Cofounders: PG, Jessica Livingston, Robert Tappan Morris, and Trevor Blackwell.
- Sources: [paulgraham.com/ycstart.html](https://paulgraham.com/ycstart.html), [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/paul-graham-startup-school-radio-interview/)

**Decision logic:**
- YC arose from systematically reconsidering his painful Viaweb experience of fundraising and inadequate guidance.
- Backing hackers over suits meant valuing the founder's technical ability over the business plan.
- That defied an industry preference for MBAs and “mature” business plans.

### 3.2 Inventing batches by accident

**Key facts:**
- PG did not design two annual three-month intensive programs concluding with Demo Day in advance.
- They originally planned conventional asynchronous angel investing, one deal at a time.
- Funding a batch was a hack that let them learn investing quickly.
- PG later realized they had accidentally brought mass-production techniques to a traditionally handmade industry.
- Sources: [Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/y-combinator-paul-graham/), [paulgraham.com/ycstart.html](https://paulgraham.com/ycstart.html)

**Graham's account:**
- "Originally it was going to be like regular angel investing—asynchronous, not the batch model—but we discovered the batch approach by accident."
- After that they "started trying to do it on purpose".

**Decision logic:**
- Find what works through execution and expand it deliberately instead of designing the ideal system first.
- Batches give founders peers, reduce costs through standardization, and concentrate attention at Demo Day.
- Outside observation: Hundreds of accelerators copied the model, turning it into a standard.

### 3.3 Investment criteria: People before ideas

**Key facts:**
- Criteria: Determination, flexibility, imagination, naughtiness, and friendship between cofounders.
- Intelligence is not on the list. Beyond a threshold, determination matters much more to PG.
- "If a group of founders seemed impressive enough, I'd fund them with no idea."
- He favors teams of two or three, accepting but not encouraging solo founders.
- YC accepts only 3%.
- Sources: [Capitaly](https://www.capitaly.vc/blog/top-y-combinator-startups-applying-paul-grahams-investment-philosophy), [gavrilobozovic.com](https://www.gavrilobozovic.com/thoughts/paul-graham)

**Decision logic:**
- Choosing founders over ideas ran against evaluating plans and market size.
- Strong founders can pivot into a good idea, but weak founders can ruin even an excellent one.
- "Naughtiness" fits PG's preference for people who break rules and challenge authority.

### 3.4 Prominent YC investments

**Key successes:**

| Company | Valuation or IPO | PG's role or story |
|---|---|---|
| **Airbnb** | 2020 IPO, over $100 billion in market value | PG advised "Do things that don't scale". Founders personally took photos and met hosts |
| **Stripe** | $107 billion valuation | Patrick and John Collison |
| **Dropbox** | 2018 IPO, $9 billion | Drew Houston |
| **Reddit** | 2024 IPO, $6.4 billion | Jessica Livingston rejected it initially, then admitted it |
| **Coinbase** | 2021 IPO, $86 billion | — |
| **DoorDash** | Public company | — |
| **Twitch** | Acquired by Amazon | — |
| **Instacart** | Public company | — |

- By 2025 YC had invested in 5,000+ companies worth a combined $600 billion.
- Sources: [Wikipedia/Y_Combinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Combinator), [Eqvista](https://eqvista.com/top-100-y-combinator-companies/), [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/business/y-combinator-venture-model/)

### 3.5 Misses and rejections

**Known cases:**
- Reddit: At first rejected, leaving Livingston dejected, then admitted. YC's best-known near miss.
- VEED.IO: Rejected after its 2019 final interview. Sequoia invested $35 million in 2022. It now has $50 million in annual revenue and 100,000 paying users. A clear miss for YC.
- WakeMate/Perfect Third: YC invested, but the hardware literally caught fire and the company failed.
- Source: [Inc.com](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201309/issie-lapowsky/how-paul-graham-became-successful.html)

**Outside observation:**
- PG rarely names investment regrets in public. That may be deliberate because discussing misses can harm an investor's brand.
- He has repeatedly told the Reddit story as a cautionary lesson about trusting one's instincts.

## IV. Stepping away from YC's daily management (2014)

### 4.1 How the decision happened

**Key facts:**
- In February 2014 Graham announced that he would cease running YC day to day.
- Sam Altman, a YC partner since 2011, succeeded him as president.
- Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/02/21/sam-altman-taking-over-as-president-of-y-combinator-replacing-paul-graham-at-the-helm/), [Inc.com](https://www.inc.com/alyson-shontell/paul-graham-will-no-longer-lead-y-combinator.html)

**Graham's account:**
- "YC needs to grow, and I'm not the best person to grow it."
- "I'm just not much good at running the sort of (comparatively) large organization YC is going to have to become."
- "Sam is what YC needs at this stage in its evolution."
- Source: [Inc.com](https://www.inc.com/alyson-shontell/paul-graham-will-no-longer-lead-y-combinator.html)

**Graham on the broader trend:**
- "We are in the beginning of a secular change in the number of startups. Starting a startup is becoming a normal thing to do... for very ambitious people, it's going to be something they at least consider doing."
- Source: As above.

**Decision logic:**
- This shows self-knowledge. Graham excels at intensive early-stage mentoring on a small scale, not managing large organizations.
- The choice of Altman was contentious and consequential. He expanded YC, but his OpenAI involvement later complicated the discussion of conflicts of interest.
- Outside observation: Leaving in 2014 was wise. Few founders accurately judge when to give way. Most depart too early or too late.

### 4.2 What he did afterward

- Remained an adviser to YC and continued attending office hours.
- Focused on writing and published some of his most influential essays after 2014.
- Moved to England in 2016 (see below).
- Continued angel investing.

## V. Sam Altman and OpenAI

### 5.1 Key facts

- Altman cofounded OpenAI in 2015 with Elon Musk, Peter Thiel, Jessica Livingston, and others. It began as a nonprofit.
- YC's later-stage fund invested $10 million in OpenAI's for-profit subsidiary. Graham says this preceded Altman's full-time move to OpenAI and that Graham himself was unaware of it.
- In 2019, after the for-profit subsidiary was formed and Altman became CEO, Livingston told him to choose. Doing OpenAI full-time meant finding someone else to run YC. He agreed.
- Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/30/paul-graham-claims-altman-wasnt-fired-from-y-combinator/), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2023/11/22/sam-altman-fired-y-combinator-paul-graham/)

### 5.2 Was Altman “fired”?

- After OpenAI's board briefly dismissed him in late 2023, former board members suggested YC had fired him previously.
- Graham denied this publicly and said Altman agreed to leave.
- Outside observation: The truth may lie in between. Being required to choose can have much the same practical effect as being asked to leave.

## VI. Moving to England (2016)

### 6.1 Key facts

- In 2016 Graham moved to England with his family, expecting to stay one year so his children could experience another country.
- Born in England, he had always wanted to live there again.
- They came to like it and stayed.
- He lives in the countryside rather than London.
- He tweeted in 2021, "Today's our 5th anniversary of moving back to England".
- Sources: [X/Twitter @paulg](https://x.com/paulg/status/1408336840881487877), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37072542)

**Graham's account:**
- One word for why he likes England: "calmer".
- He also shared pictures of the beautiful English countryside, describing it as the default rural landscape.
- Source: [X/Twitter @paulg](https://x.com/paulg/status/1665008505072263173)

**Decision logic:**
- This is typical of Graham. Try it without committing permanently, stay if you like it.
- Moving to England was a departure from the Silicon Valley model. Rather than criticize the Valley directly, he expressed his preference in action.
- Outside observation: Leaving in 2016 was unusual, four years before COVID-era remote work. It sits in tension with his advice to work in a startup hub, but he did not need one anymore.
- **Consistency**: He wrote "Why to Move to a Startup Hub" and moved to rural England. Not necessarily a contradiction. His advice was for startup founders, and he was now a writer and investor.

## VII. The Arc programming language: PG's most visible failure

### 7.1 Timeline

- 2001: PG announced Arc, a new Lisp dialect.
- January 29, 2008: The first public Arc release appeared, based on Racket.
- Hacker News, YC's community forum, was written in Arc.
- Development slowed markedly and community members began making unofficial forks.
- 2024: HN migrated its underlying implementation from Racket to SBCL (Common Lisp), indicating that Arc itself was no longer the priority.
- Sources: [Wikipedia/Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_(programming_language)), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/05/18/the-evolution-of-hacker-news/)

### 7.2 Why build Arc?

**Graham's account:**
- He wanted a simpler Lisp. Lisp's core ideas were right, but its implementations were bloated.
- Arc was “not ready for real projects.” He released it to collect feedback from hackers.
- Sources: [paulgraham.com/arc.html](https://paulgraham.com/arc.html), [paulgraham.com/arcfaq.html](https://paulgraham.com/arcfaq.html)

### 7.3 Why he mentioned it less later

**Outside criticism:**
- Lack of libraries: "Arc is so lacking in libraries".
- Poor performance: It was slower than Python, limiting real-world use.
- Lack of sustained updates: The official branch was inactive for long stretches.
- Sources: [righto.com](http://www.righto.com/2008/05/why-arc-is-bad-for-exploratory.html), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9270478)

**Decision logic:**
- Arc is a rare project PG announced publicly but did not deliver on.
- The seven years between announcement in 2001 and release in 2008 raised expectations too high.
- PG may have underestimated the gap between creating a language and creating a web app. The former requires an enduring ecosystem, while the latter can succeed through individual effort.
- Outside observation: Arc's real value was **Hacker News**, the byproduct written in Arc that had much greater influence than the language itself.
- **PG's handling**: No formal announcement of abandoning Arc and no conspicuous admission of failure. He mentioned it less and less. This quiet withdrawal is characteristic of how he handles failure.

## VIII. PG's statements and actions in the AI era

### 8.1 Key views

**“Not every new company needs to do AI” (2025):**
- Graham pushed back on the AI boom when AI startups raised $104.3 billion in the first half of 2025.
- The two most impressive companies in the current YC batch **were not AI companies**, he said.
- "Founders matter more than the idea. The founders are the best predictor of how a company will do, not the industry it's in."
- Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/18/yc-co-founder-paul-graham-not-every-new-company-needs-to-be-about-ai.html)

**Specific advice for AI founders (2024–2025):**
- "AI is turning out to be the missing piece in a large number of important, almost-completed puzzles." It is not a solution searching for a problem but the final piece of many existing puzzles.
- Measure weekly growth (5–10% or more), not the absolute number of users.
- Beware the hype cycle. Private capital chasing AI may make fundraising easier, but "VCs want to blow you up, one way or the other".
- Source: [charlesandsystems.substack.com](https://charlesandsystems.substack.com/p/paul-grahams-playbook-for-ai-startup)

**"Writes and Write-Nots" (2024):**
- Predicts AI will divide people into those who can write and those who cannot.
- "Writing is thinking" — abandoning writing means giving up learning to think clearly.
- Source: As above.

**AI and programming work:**
- Graham sees entry-level programming roles as especially vulnerable.
- Become indispensable by solving complex, creative problems AI cannot yet handle.
- Source: [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/paul-graham-ai-disrupting-entry-level-coding-jobs-embrace-innovation/)

### 8.2 Investments

- Graham personally backed an AI pesticide company with teenage founders ($6 million round, 2025).
- YC's late-stage fund invested $10 million in OpenAI (timing disputed).
- Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/teen-founders-raise-6m-to-reinvent-pesticides-using-ai-and-convince-paul-graham-to-join-in/)

### 8.3 A revealing post

- Graham wrote on X: "An encouraging thought about AI: If the most influential readers of my essays in the future are AIs, and AIs are smart and rational, I don't have to worry about the way stupid or biased readers will react to what I write. I can safely aim high."
- The post suggests optimism about AI readers. He thinks they will be more rational than human ones.
- Source: [X/Twitter @paulg](https://x.com/paulg/status/2025588288946413710)

## IX. The "Founder Mode" essay (September 2024)

### 9.1 Key facts

- Published September 1, 2024, it quickly became one of Silicon Valley's hottest topics.
- Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky's talk at a YC event inspired it. Many told PG it was the best talk they had heard.
- Chesky recounted how he followed conventional wisdom by hiring and delegating, nearly wrecking the company.
- He studied Steve Jobs's management and switched to founder mode.
- Sources: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/09/01/paul-graham-founder-mode-silicon-valley-conventional-wisdom-manager-mode/), [paulgraham.com/foundermode.html](https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html)

### 9.2 Core concept

- **Founder mode versus manager mode**: Founders get into the details themselves versus hiring professional managers and delegating.
- PG believes the conventional advice to "hire good people and give them room to do their jobs" **is wrong**.
- Source: [Inc.com](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/what-is-founder-mode-y-combinators-paul-graham-says-it-can-make-or-break-your-business.html)

### 9.3 Decision logic

- This essay is a reflection on **delegation**, in interesting contrast with PG's leaving YC and handing it to Sam Altman.
- Outside observation: Some point to his handover as a classic instance of delegation. PG would say he did not hire a professional manager but found another founder type.
- **Impact of the essay**: It gave micromanaging founders a justification: “I'm not a control freak. I'm using founder mode.”

## X. Consistency Between Words and Actions

### 10.1 Where They Align

| PG's advice | His own practice |
|---------|------------|
| Make something people want | Viaweb pivoted from a failed art-gallery website to online stores. |
| Do things that don't scale | Viaweb helped early customers build their websites by hand. |
| Prize determination | His own Viaweb nearly died several times but survived. |
| Writing is thinking | He has written essays for 30 years without stopping. |
| Founders should know when to step aside | He voluntarily handed over YC in 2014. |
| Don't let a big company tie you down | He left Yahoo less than a year after selling Viaweb. |

### 10.2 Where There Is Tension

| PG's advice | Potential contradiction |
|---------|---------|
| “Move to a startup hub” | He moved to rural England in 2016, although he was no longer running a startup. |
| Advocate frugality | His estimated net worth is about $2.5 billion, although his advice concerns the early stages of a startup. |
| “Founder Mode” (founders should be deeply involved) | He handed YC to Altman, but saw that as finding another founder rather than hiring a manager. |
| Criticize big-company culture | YC itself has become a large institution. |
| Emphasize “Mean People Fail” | Some critics see his stance on economic inequality as arrogant. |

### 10.3 Summary of Outside Criticism

**Economic inequality (controversy prompted by his 2016 essay)**
- Graham's argument is that some economic inequality stems from startups creating value, and that this kind of inequality is good.
- Critics counter that:
  - Graham wrongly assumes everyone has equal opportunities to start a company.
  - As someone who controls access to startup capital, Graham has “plutocratic authority.”
  - The extreme concentration of wealth in Silicon Valley has driven up housing prices and indirectly harmed communities.
  - Graham's argument contains a conflict of interest. He himself is a leading beneficiary of the “startups are good” narrative.
- Sources: [Quartz](https://qz.com/586563/paul-graham-just-accidentally-explained-everything-wrong-with-silicon-valleys-world-view), [Inc.com](https://www.inc.com/jeff-bercovici/paul-graham-wrong-inequality.html), [Medium/Dare Obasanjo](https://dareobasanjo.medium.com/what-paul-graham-got-wrong-about-income-inequality-198bdf5d9734)

## XI. Failures and Acknowledged Mistakes

### 11.1 Clear Failures

1. **The Arc programming language:** Seven years of development, high expectations, and little delivered. Its own by-product, Hacker News, eventually outshone it. Graham never formally admitted failure and instead quietly stepped away.
2. **The art-gallery website (Viaweb's predecessor):** Graham called it “a stupid idea.” It is the failure he acknowledged most frankly.
3. **Viaweb's repeated brushes with closure in its early years:** These were threats to survival during execution rather than failures of judgment.

### 11.2 How Graham Reflects on His Own Work

- Graham rarely **admits a specific error in judgment head-on**.
- He tends to reframe failures as learning experiences. For example, the gallery website failed, and from that failure came YC's motto.
- His essays often say “I used to think X, then I discovered Y,” but these describe **changes in understanding**, not **specific mistakes**.
- Outside observation: This approach is both a strength, because it sustains his image as a thought leader, and a limitation, because he seldom shares failure in its raw form.

### 11.3 Errors in Judgment Identified by Others

- His analysis of economic inequality was widely criticized as oversimplified.
- Some critics say Graham champions startups too strongly and overlooks that valuable work need not take the form of a startup.
- Some read “Founder Mode” as a justification for micromanagement.
- Sources: See section X.

---

## XII. Summary of Decision Patterns

Across these cases, Graham's decisions show several recurring patterns:

1. **Act before the plan is perfect:** Viaweb grew out of a pivot from the gallery website, and YC discovered its batch model by accident.
2. **Leave unsuitable environments quickly:** He left Yahoo within a year and voluntarily handed over YC in 2014.
3. **Show preferences through actions rather than arguing:** He moved to England rather than writing an essay against Silicon Valley, and let Arc go quiet rather than declaring it a failure.
4. **Redefine failure as learning:** The gallery's failure led to “Make something people want.” Viaweb's brushes with closure taught him what it takes for a startup to survive.
5. **Trust people more than plans:** “Fund people not ideas” is a philosophy of life as well as investing for him.
6. **Keep several pursuits going at once:** He never did only one thing. Programming, painting, writing, and investing ran in parallel.
7. **Know your limits:** He recognized that leading a large organization did not suit him and stepped aside in time.
