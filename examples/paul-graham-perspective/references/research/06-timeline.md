## Timeline overview

| Period | Year | Event | Influence on his thinking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Birth and youth** | 1964.11.13 | Born in Weymouth, Dorset, England | British-born and American-raised, with exposure to both cultures |
| | 1968 | Moved with his family to Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania | Grew up in an American industrial city |
| | High school | Began coding | Early programming laid the foundation of his hacker identity |
| **University: philosophy** | 1986 | Earned a BA in philosophy from Cornell University | Philosophy trained him in clear argumentation and first-principles thinking. Coming from a non-CS undergraduate background helped him retain an outsider perspective. |
| **Harvard doctorate** | 1988 | Earned an MS in computer science from Harvard University | |
| | 1990 | Earned a PhD in computer science from Harvard University | Lisp-related research at Harvard gave him a deep understanding of Lisp and the conviction that programming languages shape thought. |
| **Studying painting** | ~1990–1993 | Studied painting at the Rhode Island School of Design (RISD) | Art training helped him understand how makers work and directly informed the central analogy in “Hackers and Painters.” |
| | ~1993 | Studied painting at the Accademia di Belle Arti in Florence, Italy, at about 25 | While studying in the city of the Renaissance, he found that contemporary Florence was no longer an artistic center. In “Cities and Ambition,” he later argued that the character of a city shapes who you become. |
| **Lisp books** | 1993 | Published *On Lisp* | It established his standing in the Lisp community and was his first systematic account of using a programming language as a tool for thinking. |
| | 1995 | Published *ANSI Common Lisp* | The two technical books made him a prominent figure in the Lisp world. |
| **Viaweb** | 1995.07 | Co-founded Viaweb with Robert Morris and Trevor Blackwell | Viaweb, described here as the first web application and a precursor to SaaS, used browsers to build online stores. Writing commercial software in Lisp was unconventional, but tested his theory that a powerful language confers an advantage. |
| | 1996 | Viaweb went live | The company started with $10,000 in seed funding from a lawyer friend and avoided venture capital. Bootstrapping later shaped YC's emphasis on frugality. |
| | 1997 | Viaweb hosted 500 stores | |
| | 1998 | Viaweb hosted 1,000 stores | |
| **Yahoo acquisition** | Summer 1998 | Yahoo acquired Viaweb for 455,000 Yahoo shares, worth about $49.6 million, and renamed it Yahoo Store | His first major exit. He experienced the cultural clash that followed a large company's acquisition of a startup. |
| **Yahoo period** | 1998–1999 | Left Yahoo after about one year | The experience convinced him that large companies stifle innovation, a lesson that recurs in his later startup writing. |
| **Interlude and transition to writing** | 1999–2001 | Tried to keep painting after Yahoo, then gradually turned to writing | He realized he had more aptitude and enthusiasm for writing. “Do what you love” described a life transition he had experienced. |
| **Prolific essay writing** | 2001 | Began posting essays at paulgraham.com. Announced Arc at the LL1 conference when it was only three weeks into development. | Pioneered a form of long essays by programmers. His work was carefully revised nonfiction, beyond ordinary blog posts. |
| | 2001 | Published “The Other Road Ahead,” anticipating web applications, smartphones, and cloud data | Anticipated the form of the mobile internet six years before the iPhone. |
| | 2002 | Published “Revenge of the Nerds,” listing nine features of Lisp | |
| | 2003 | Published “Hackers and Painters,” drawing an analogy between hackers and painters | |
| | 2003 | Published “A Plan for Spam,” proposing Bayesian spam filtering | The essay directly affected the anti-spam industry's technical direction. |
| **Publishing a book** | 2004 | Published *Hackers & Painters: Big Ideas from the Computer Age* through O'Reilly | The collected essays established his standing as a Silicon Valley thinker. |
| | 2005.03 | Gave “How to Start a Startup” to the Harvard Computer Society | The talk directly helped give rise to YC. |
| **Founding YC** | 2005.03.11 | Came up with the idea for YC while walking with Jessica Livingston | Livingston was working in investment banking and disliked it. Graham's ideas and her execution complemented each other. |
| | 2005.03 | Co-founded Y Combinator with Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell, launched the site, and started the Summer Founders Program | YC redefined startup investing with batches, short cycles, small seed checks, and more emphasis on founders than business plans. The central insight was that the founders matter more than the idea. |
| | Summer 2005 | YC's first batch funded eight startups, including Reddit | He saw the power of batch investing almost immediately: founders learn from one another, creating a network effect. |
| **YC growth** | 2007 | Created Hacker News using Arc | It became a central community for Silicon Valley founders and programmers and a major channel for Graham's ideas. |
| | 2008.01.29 | Released the first public Arc version, based on PLT Scheme/Racket | Reviews of Arc were mixed, but Hacker News itself was the best demonstration of the language. |
| | 2008 | Married Jessica Livingston | His business partner became his life partner. |
| | ~2009–2012 | YC invested in future major companies including Dropbox, Airbnb, and Stripe | YC grew from an experiment into Silicon Valley's most influential incubator. |
| **YC succession** | Mid-2012 | Began looking for a successor, with Sam Altman as the only candidate | |
| | 2014.02 | Formally stepped down from daily operations, and Sam Altman took over as YC president | Graham said, “YC needs to grow, and I'm not the best person to grow it.” He acted on his belief in doing what one does well. |
| | 2014.03 | Completed the transition after Winter 2014 Demo Day | He continued holding office hours for YC founders and stepped away from daily administration. |
| **Return to writing** | 2014–2016 | Refocused on writing and produced many essays | He went from operator back to thinker, an example of his theory that one should do what one does best and most enjoys. |
| **Move back to Britain** | 2016 | Settled in Britain with Jessica and their two children | His birth country offered a base for thinking and writing away from the noise of Silicon Valley. |
| **Continued writing** | 2019 | Published “The Bus Ticket Theory of Genius” | Genius comes from obsessive interest in something, beyond IQ alone. |
| | 2021 | Reduced his activities for health reasons that were kept private | |
| | 2023.07 | Published “How to Do Great Work,” a long essay on producing great work | Possibly his most comprehensive essay, combining decades of thought on creativity. |
| | 2023.10 | Published “Superlinear Returns” | The modern returns curve is superlinear. Returns are disproportionate to performance, and winner-take-all effects are expanding. |
| **2024: Founder Mode** | 2024.03 | Published “How to Start Google,” “The Best Essay,” and “Superlinear Returns” | |
| | 2024.07 | Published “The Reddits” | |
| | 2024.09.02 | Published “The Right Kind of Stubborn” | Distinguishes good stubbornness, holding firmly to the goal while adapting methods, from bad stubbornness. |
| | 2024.09.09 | Published “Founder Mode,” inspired by Brian Chesky's experience at Airbnb | The essay sparked debate in Silicon Valley. Its core argument was that founders should remain deeply involved, taking cues from Jobs instead of handing management to professional managers. |
| | 2024.10 | Published “When to Do What You Love” | |

Society will divide into those who can write and those who cannot. Writing is thinking, so giving up writing means giving up thinking. |

## Intellectual turning points

| Turning point | Trigger | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Philosophy to computer science** | Cornell philosophy BA → Harvard CS PhD | Humanities perspective | The combination of philosophy and programming kept him asking why as well as how. |
| **Academia to painting** | Studied at RISD and in Florence after the PhD | Academic path | Understood how makers discover what they are doing through the work itself. |
| **Painting to entrepreneurship** | Founded Viaweb | Artist | Founder who kept a maker's philosophy of work. |
| **Entrepreneurship to writing** | Left Yahoo | Doer | Thinker and writer who saw the essay as his medium. |
| **Personal writing to institution-building** | Founded YC | Independent thinker | Tried to teach his startup experience systematically and at scale. |
| **Institution-building to independent work** | Stepped down from YC | Organizational manager | Returned to independent thought after realizing he was unsuited to managing growth. |
| **Silicon Valley to Britain** | Moved in 2016 | At the center of Silicon Valley | Physical distance created intellectual distance. Away from the noise, essays became deeper, slower, and more fundamental. |
| **A new role in the AI era** | 2024–2026 AI wave | Focus on startups and programming | Began exploring AI's effects on writing, thought, taste, and startups. His central view is that taste outranks execution and writing ability equates to thinking ability. |

## Family (public information)

- **Wife:** Jessica Livingston, YC co-founder and author of *Founders at Work*.
- **Children:** Two. Their names and birth years are private.
- **Marriage:** 2008.
- **Residence:** Britain, his country of birth, since 2016.
- Graham regards Jessica as YC's social glue. She interviewed and screened founders, and he has said publicly many times that YC undervalues her central role.

## Notes on the data

- @paulg remains active on X and continued posting in 2026.
- He publishes an essay about every four to eight weeks, maintaining a steady output in 2024–2025.
- The 2025 essays focus noticeably on AI's effects on human thought, including “Writes and Write-Nots.”
- The 2026 X posts indicate he still lives in Britain and follows the relationship between AI and taste.
- YC has invested in more than 1,300 startups. Graham no longer participates in day-to-day operations and retains influence.
