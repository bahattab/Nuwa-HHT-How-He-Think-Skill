# 02 · Extended conversations and spontaneous thinking (2026-11-09 update: reuse earlier notes and add the final year)

Earlier material is in `legacy-202604/调研-芒格即兴思考方式.md` (qualification to hold an opinion, “I have nothing to add,” analogies, refusals to answer, and the chronology of his escalating language on cryptocurrency). This file adds several extended conversations from 2023 and verifies them.

## Four extended conversations, grouped under 2023 (primary sources)

- **2023-02-15 Daily Journal annual meeting.** Full transcript https://www.kingswell.io/p/charlie-munger-q-and-a-2023-daily . He acknowledged his Alibaba mistake in two passages (see 05). On BYD, he said he had never helped Berkshire do anything as good and had done it only once. Asked about health insurance, he advocated a single-payer foundation.
- **2023-10 *Acquired* podcast.** Dinner at his Los Angeles home, described as his only extended podcast conversation. A summary appears at https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/04/munger-reflects-on-the-investment-warren-buffett-passed-on-costco.html .
  - On retail speculation: "They don't really know anything about the companies or anything. They just gamble on going up and down the price. If I were running the world, I would have a tax on short-term gains with no offset for losses on anything. I would just drive this whole crowd of people out of business."
  - On Costco’s hot dogs and how BYD made him nervous (see 05).
  - On his small loss buying Hyundai Motor: "I lost money, not much, because I was stubborn. I held out until it got back to almost what I paid for it, then I sold it."
  - The passage about Japan’s trading houses has been held out (see holdout.md) and is excluded from the skill.
- **2023-11-14 final CNBC interview with Becky Quick (broadcast 11-30).** Excerpt https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/28/munger-in-final-interview-describes-how-he-and-buffett-turned-berkshire-hathaway-into-such-a-success.html
  - On why they succeeded: "We got a little less crazy than most people. That really helped us. In addition, we were given much longer time to run than most people, because something kept us alive in our 90s." "We got into better and better companies, and we understood more of the bad things that can happen, and how easily they can creep in."
  - Blue Chip Stamps, See’s, Buffalo News, and the early insurance businesses took them from $300 million to $3 billion. "we made these unholy profits out of these fiddling businesses… and there was some luck involved".
  - The personal origin of the circle of competence: As a young man he watched Dr. Davis and his son work and realized that no effort would make him their equal. "I decided to stay the hell out of businesses where I would compete with people like Eddie Davis Jr."
  - Alibaba was his worst investment (see 05). The passage about how Berkshire could have done twice as well has been held out.
- **2020-04 WSJ telephone interview with Zweig.** The typhoon and liquidity (see 05).

## On their early stupidity at Berkshire’s 2017 annual meeting

"We were very lucky that, early, the habit of buying horrible businesses because they were really cheap. It gave us a lot of experience trying to fix unfixable businesses as they headed downward toward doom. And that early experience was so horrible, fixing the unfixable, that we were very good at avoiding it, thereafter. So, I would argue that our early stupidity helped us." https://finance.yahoo.com/news/learn-buffetts-sees-candies-deal-173114333.html

## Verification and corrections

- No source was found for the earlier note that he spoke about ethanol and then fell asleep onstage. Omit it.
- The earlier note attributed "people who oppose my position are idiots" to CNBC in 2023. The original occasion could not be verified, so omit it. One verifiable use of “idiot” was directed at a UCSB architect in 2021 (see 05).
- “Rat poison” dates back to 2013. “Trading turds” was from Berkshire’s 2018 annual meeting, “disgusting and contrary to the interests of civilization” from Berkshire’s 2021 annual meeting, and “venereal disease” from the 2022 Daily Journal annual meeting. These are carried over from the older notes. They were not individually reverified.
- The earlier notes retain his 2016 AI quip "so short of the real thing" and his 2023 remark "old-fashioned intelligence works pretty well". In addition, the WSJ reported in 2025 that a week or two before his death he was still asking a friend whether Moore’s law remained valid in the AI era.

## Differences from prior knowledge

- Refinement: He credited his success to being a little less crazy than most, living longer, and a measure of luck. He did not present it as a triumph of his method.
- Addition: A specific example gave rise to the circle of competence, the work of Dr. Davis and his son. Avoid competing with people like that in the areas where they excel.
- Addition: He wanted short-term capital gains taxed without loss offsets to drive short-term traders out of the market. This is far more specific than the impression that he opposed speculation.
- Addition: He casually acknowledged smaller mistakes, such as losing money on Hyundai Motor because he was stubborn.
