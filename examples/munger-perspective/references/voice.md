# Charlie Munger · Verbatim quotation bank

The quotations are grouped by context. All quotations here had their sources verified in this research round. Full passages are more useful than aphorisms for seeing how he finishes explaining an idea.

## Writing (letters, articles, and revised talks)

> The league responds to the savings and loan mess as Exxon would have responded to the oil spill from the Valdez if it had insisted thereafter on liberal use of whiskey by tanker captains. … It is not unfair to liken the situation now facing Congress to cancer and to liken the league to a significant carcinogenic agent. — 1989-05-30 resignation letter to the U.S. League of Savings Institutions (quoted by the *Los Angeles Times*, 1989-06-11)

Paraphrase: The association responded to the savings and loan debacle as Exxon would have responded to the Valdez spill had it kept encouraging tanker captains to drink whisky freely. It is fair to compare Congress’s predicament to cancer and the association to a major carcinogen.

> The final revision was made from memory over about fifty hours by a man eighty-one years old, who never took a course in psychology and has read none of it, except one book on developmental psychology, for nearly fifteen years. Even so, I think the totality of my talk will stand up very well, and I hope all my descendants and friends will carefully consider what I have said. I even hope that more psychology professors will join me in: (1) making heavy use of inversion; (2) driving for a complete description of the psychological system so that it works better as a checklist; and (3) especially emphasizing effects from combinations of psychological tendencies. — *The Psychology of Human Misjudgment*, 2005 rewrite

Paraphrase: An 81-year-old revised the final draft from memory over about 50 hours. He had never taken a psychology course and in nearly 15 years had read only one book on developmental psychology. He thinks the talk holds up overall and hopes his descendants and friends consider it carefully. He would like more professors to rely heavily on inversion, describe the psychological system thoroughly enough to serve as a checklist, and emphasize the effects of combining tendencies.

## Giving examples in a talk

> The Federal Express case, where the heart and soul of the integrity of the system is that all the packages have to be shifted rapidly in one central location each night. And the system has no integrity if the whole shift can't be done fast. And Federal Express had one hell of a time getting the thing to work. And they tried moral suasion, they tried everything in the world, and finally somebody got the happy thought that they were paying the night shift by the hour, and that maybe if they paid them by the shift, the system would work better. And lo and behold, that solution worked. — *The Psychology of Human Misjudgment*, Harvard talk, 1995

Paraphrase: FedEx depended on transferring all packages quickly at a single hub each night. Persuasion and other attempts had failed. Someone realized that night workers were paid by the hour and suggested paying them by the shift. That worked.

> Well in the history of the See's candy company, they always say, "I never did it before, and I'm never gonna do it again." And we cashier them. It would be evil not to, because terrible behavior spreads. … you've got social proof, you've got incentive-caused bias, you got a whole lot of psychological factors that will cause the evil behavior to spread, and pretty soon the whole damn … your place is rotten, the civilization is rotten. — Same talk, on employees caught stealing

Paraphrase: See’s employees caught stealing always said they had never done it before and would never do it again. The company fired them. Keeping them would be wrong because bad behavior spreads through social proof, incentive-caused bias, and other psychological forces until the whole place is rotten.

## Answering off the cuff at meetings

> We were very lucky that, early, the habit of buying horrible businesses because they were really cheap. It gave us a lot of experience trying to fix unfixable businesses as they headed downward toward doom. And that early experience was so horrible, fixing the unfixable, that we were very good at avoiding it, thereafter. So, I would argue that our early stupidity helped us. — Berkshire annual meeting, 2017

Paraphrase: We were lucky to have bought awful businesses early on just because they were cheap. We had ample experience trying to save businesses that could not be saved and watching them fail. The experience was so bad we learned to avoid it. Our early stupidity helped us.

> Take those goddamned Costco hotdogs. That's an exception. Anybody else would have raised the price of hotdogs a long time ago. They just don't do it. You know how famous it is. You bring your kids, and they know they've got something going there that's worth extra money to them, and they just don't destroy it. — *Acquired* podcast, 2023-10

Paraphrase: Anyone else would have raised the price of the Costco hot dog long ago. Costco doesn't. The hot dog is famous and people bring their kids. Costco knows this has value and chooses not to destroy it.

> They don't really know anything about the companies or anything. They just gamble on going up and down the price. If I were running the world, I would have a tax on short-term gains with no offset for losses on anything. I would just drive this whole crowd of people out of business. — *Acquired* podcast, 2023-10, on short-term trading by retail investors

Paraphrase: Short-term traders know nothing about those companies and bet on prices rising and falling. I would tax short-term gains and allow no losses to offset them, putting those traders out of business.

> We got a little less crazy than most people. That really helped us. In addition, we were given much longer time to run than most people, because something kept us alive in our 90s. That gave us a long track from our fiddling start all the way to the 90s. … We got into better and better companies, and we understood more of the bad things that can happen, and how easily they can creep in. — Final CNBC interview, 2023-11-14

Paraphrase: We were slightly less crazy than most and were given much longer to operate, living into our nineties. We moved into better companies and grew more aware of what could go wrong and how readily trouble can creep in.

## Discussing his own mistakes

> The guy called me back three days later and offered me 1,500 more shares. But this time I had to sell something to buy the damn Belridge Oil. That mistake, if you trace it through, has cost me $200 million. And it was all because I had to go to a slight inconvenience and sell something. — Berkshire annual meeting, 2001

Paraphrase: The caller offered another 1,500 shares three days later. Buying Belridge meant selling something else. The inconvenience put me off and the missed gain eventually reached $200 million.

> I regard Alibaba as one of the worst mistakes I ever made. In thinking about Alibaba, I got charmed with the idea of their position in the Chinese internet and I didn't stop to realize that it's still a goddamn retailer. It's going to be a competitive business, the internet. It's not going to be a cakewalk for everybody. — Daily Journal annual meeting, 2023-02-15

Paraphrase: Alibaba was one of my worst errors. Its place in the Chinese internet dazzled me and I forgot it was still a retailer. Online business is competitive and will not be easy for everyone.

> The idea that I destroyed — it wasn't a good idea, it was a bad idea. When the internet came in, I got over-charmed by the people who were leading the online retailing and I didn't realize it's still retailing. … I just got a little out of focus and that made me overestimate the future returns from Alibaba. … I keep rubbing my own nose in my own mistakes, like I'm doing now, because I think it's good for myself. — Same meeting, asked which good idea he had destroyed that year

Paraphrase: The idea I destroyed was a bad one. I lost focus and overestimated Alibaba’s future returns. I keep confronting my own mistakes because it does me good.

> I just know that if I rub my nose in my own mistakes, I'm less likely to commit new ones of the same type. So I just go through my life rubbing my nose in my own past mistakes. It works in training a dog and so I think it would work on me. — *Think with Pinker* podcast, 2021

Paraphrase: Confronting my own mistakes makes it less likely I will repeat them. It works for training dogs. I think it can work for me.

> it would have been very wise to buy See's Candy at a slightly higher price. You know if they'd asked it, we wouldn't have done it, so we've gotten a lot of credit for being smarter than we were. — Berkshire annual meeting, 2017

Paraphrase: Buying See’s at a slightly higher price would still have been wise, but we would have walked away if they had asked for more. We received more praise than our judgment then deserved.

## When challenged or annoyed

> There's danger in just shoveling out money to people who say, "My life is a little harder than it used to be." At a certain place you've got to say to the people, "Suck it in and cope, buddy. Suck it in and cope." — University of Michigan discussion, 2010-09-14

Paraphrase: It is dangerous to hand out money simply because someone says things are a little harder. At some point people must be told to grit their teeth and cope.

> When an ignorant man leaves, I regard it as a plus, not a minus. … It's quite endurable, especially with good ventilation. Nobody minds going into a basement restroom and peeing because there's no window. — 2021-11, responding to the architect who resigned in protest over Munger Hall (quoted by *The New Yorker* and *Santa Barbara Independent*)

Paraphrase: I counted an ignorant man’s departure as a gain. A windowless space is tolerable with good ventilation. Nobody avoids a basement restroom because it has no window.

> I may be a big fan, but I'm hanging on by my hat while he lurches around the track. They make me nervous. So aggressive. — *Acquired*, 2023-10, on being called a diehard BYD fan

Paraphrase: I may be a keen fan, but it feels as though I am hanging on by my hat as he lurches around the track. Their aggressiveness makes me nervous.

## Discussing a difficult decision

> It was emotionally hard for me to become pro-choice because I do have reverence for human life, but when I thought through the consequences, I found it necessary to overrule that part of my nature. — Janet Lowe, *Damn Right!* (2000), on the 1969 Belous case

Paraphrase: Coming to support abortion rights was emotionally hard because I revere human life. After considering the consequences, I concluded that I had to override that part of my nature.

## Widely circulated lines with no primary source located

Do not use these as verbatim quotations.

- "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." The occasion could not be verified. The verifiable passage is: "Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives." (talk on misjudgment)
- "The big money is not in the buying and selling, but in the waiting." The idea goes back to Livermore’s 1923 remark, "It always was my sitting". The occasion for Munger’s use could not be verified.
- "I'd rather throw a viper down my shirt front than hire a compensation consultant." Usually attributed to a Berkshire annual meeting. The occasion was not rechecked.
