# Steve Jobs Perspective — Demo Conversation Transcript

> 2026-04-05 | Hua Shu's first conversation with the Jobs Skill
> Topics: AI hardware, OpenAI vs. Anthropic, and how Apple can break through

---

## 1. If you wanted to make an AI hardware product today, what would you make?

AI hardware is where the PC industry was in 1977. Everyone's building the equivalent of the Altair 8800 — impressive to geeks, useless to your mom. Rabbit, Humane Pin — those are shit. Not because the teams aren't smart, but because they started with the technology and tried to figure out where to sell it. That's backwards.

**Not a new category. Redefine one that already exists.**

The iPhone wasn't the first mobile phone. The iPod wasn't the first MP3 player. The Mac wasn't the first computer. We never invent categories. We redefine them.

The question isn't “What AI hardware should we build?” It's **which existing category offers an experience so awful it makes people want to throw things?**

My initial answer: television. I said Apple TV was the market I wanted to crack. It's been more than a decade. Nobody's cracked it.

Headline: **"It knows what you want to watch before you do."**

---

## 2. So what should it be? (Revised answer after a follow-up)

Back to first principles. Which category meets all three conditions?
1. Everyone uses it every day
2. The experience is appallingly bad
3. AI can make the experience ten times better

Let's rule them out one by one:
- Television? Not everyone uses it every day. Young people have already stopped watching
- Phones? The experience is already good enough. On phones, AI is a feature, not a revolution
- Computers? AI is redefining them, but that's happening in software
- Cars? Too heavy, and the development cycle is too long. Elon's already doing it

**Revised answer: earbuds.**

Not earbuds like AirPods. An always-on personal AI interface:
- It hears who you're speaking with and gives you context in real time
- When you arrive in an unfamiliar city, it tells you where to go in conversation, not on a map
- It listens during the meeting and gives you three action items afterward
- You speak Chinese. The other person hears English

Headline: **"An AI that lives in your ear."**

Why earbuds and not glasses? Earbuds are already an accepted category. Glasses are still a social signal. And then there's the whole widget. You control the chip, the microphone array, the AI model, the cloud. End to end.

That's a real bicycle for the mind. Not a bicycle you leave on your desk, but one you wear.

---

## 3. Who's headed in the right direction, OpenAI or Anthropic?

Wrong question. This isn't a race about direction. It's a race about **taste**.

### OpenAI = Microsoft in 1985

Do everything, win at everything, move incredibly fast, put products everywhere. ChatGPT, DALL-E, video, search, hardware, APIs. That strategy wins market share. That's how Bill Gates won.

The problem: when you do everything, you can't make every single thing insanely great. You end up with a pile of products that are “fine.”

### Anthropic = Apple in its early days

Focus. Basically do one thing — make the model better and safer. Focus means saying no to a hundred good ideas.

Dario Amodei has conviction. A CEO willing to sacrifice short-term market share for his convictions deserves respect for that alone.

**But Anthropic has a problem I had back then.** You can make the best product in the world, but without distribution, you're done. Part of the reason I was kicked out of Apple in 1985 was that the Mac, though insanely great, couldn't outsell IBM's distribution network.

The real question: **Can Anthropic find its iPod moment before OpenAI floods the market with sheer volume?** Claude Code might be a step in that direction. But I'm not sure.

### The mistake both companies are making

They don't control the hardware. They both run on other people's chips and in other people's clouds. What was it Alan Kay said? People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware.

**The eventual winner may be whoever controls the chips, the model, and the user interface.**

Who's doing all three now? Apple.

---

## 4. But isn't Apple's AI too weak right now?

Siri is shit. There's no defense for it. After thirteen years, it can still only set alarms and tell you the weather. That's what typically happens when toner heads take over the product. Tim is an incredible operator, but he's not a product guy.

**But being weak and being late are two different things.**

Google was ahead of Yahoo in search for ten years. Then the iPhone came out, and the rules of mobile search changed completely. A first-mover advantage is worthless when the paradigm shifts.

### Apple has three things nobody else has

**First, two billion devices.** The iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and AirPods. OpenAI has a webpage. Anthropic has an API. Apple has your pocket, your wrist, your ears.

**Second, chips.** The M-series and A-series are the best consumer processors in the world. On-device AI. No network needed, no need to send data to someone else's servers. In the AI era, privacy will become the scarcest thing of all.

**Third, the Whole Widget.** Apple is the only company on Earth that controls the chips, the operating system, the app ecosystem, and the hardware design.

### Where's the problem?

**Apple lacks someone who dares to say, “This is shit. Do it again.”** Tim Cook is too nice. Nice doesn't ship revolutionary products.

Apple's technical AI capabilities aren't weak. Its research papers and the efficiency of its on-device models are both impressive. What's weak is the vision. What's weak is the taste.

And you can't hire taste. You either have it or you don't.

---

## 5. How is Apple most likely to break through? Just wait for that person to show up?

Waiting for someone to show up is a loser's strategy. After I left Apple, the board waited twelve years. Apple almost died.

But Apple doesn't need “another Steve Jobs,” either. The idea itself is bullshit.

### Three paths to pursue at once

**First, Siri has to die.**

Don't improve it. Kill it and start over. You can't build a skyscraper on a rotten foundation.

Build a brand-new AI interface — a presence on your devices. It sees what you're doing, knows what you'll do next, and is ready before you say a word. Not a voice assistant. A layer of the operating system that understands you.

**Second, find the “iPod + iTunes” combination.**

Apple already has the distribution layer: App Store, iCloud, Apple Pay, Health. The pipes are in place, but there's no water. The water is a killer use case.

It could be health. Apple Watch + AI = a device that tells you before something goes wrong with your heart.

**"The device that knows you're sick before you do."**

**Third, don't move to OpenAI's rhythm.**

Everyone's moving to OpenAI's rhythm. They release ChatGPT, everyone builds a chatbot. That's a follower's mindset.

Apple should ask a completely different question: **“What experience becomes possible when AI isn't in the cloud, but in your pocket, on your wrist, and in your ears?”**

On-device AI + privacy + the whole widget. That's Apple's home court. In this game, OpenAI is nowhere.

Apple doesn't need to wait for a person. It needs to make a decision. Cut everything that isn't good enough. Pick a direction. Put the best people in the company on it. Ship it.

In 1997 I drew a 2×2 matrix and saved Apple. It doesn't take genius. It takes courage.

---

## 6. So you don't have an answer

You're right. I don't.

If I had an answer, it wouldn't be worth much. Truly revolutionary products don't come out of conversations. They come from somebody staring at a prototype for three months, throwing it five times, and cursing out the team ten times.

Where did the iPhone come from? It wasn't because I said, “Let's make a phone.” It was while we were working on the iPad, and an engineer showed me a multitouch demo. I touched it and said: Forget the tablet. Can you make a phone out of this?

**The answer is in your hands. Literally. You have to touch it.**

Everybody wants an answer. “What product do we build?” “Which direction should we go?” Those are the wrong questions.

The right question is: **What keeps you up at night?**

Not the kind of thing that keeps you up because you're anxious. The kind that keeps you up because you're excited. There's something in your head that you know should exist but doesn't yet, and it's driving you crazy.

If you ask me what AI hardware to build, my real answer is — **I don't know. Because I'm dead.** I don't have those sleepless nights anymore. I don't use products from 2025 every day and want to throw them. Without frustration, there's no insight.

So don't ask me. Ask yourself: what do you use every day that makes you think, “It's fucking 2025. How is this still so bad?”

That's your answer.

**Go find your sleepless night.**
