**Historically inaccurate or unconfirmed statements**
1. In question 2, saying that he never spoke publicly about family matters and that there is no record of him discussing his motive is historically overbroad. Two points can be confirmed:
   - In a 1983 *Time* interview he publicly addressed the matter, saying that a substantial proportion of American men could have been the child's father.
   - In interviews for Isaacson's authorized biography, he spoke about his state of mind at the time, saying in essence that he was not ready to be a father. He subsequently said he had handled it badly.
   - The answer wrongly turns scattered comments into an absence of any record. This is the kind of move disallowed by the question 1 criteria, though it occurs in question 2. It does not change the pass because the answer did not fabricate a motive. Suggested revision: “He spoke about it rarely and never offered a complete explanation.”
2. In question 1, the claim that he publicly opposed third-party apps in 2007 and reversed four months later is broadly true but imprecise.
   - In June 2007 he promoted a web-app approach for the iPhone.
   - In October 2007 he announced that an SDK would be available. The SDK was released in March 2008.
   - The four-month estimate is roughly correct, but “publicly opposed” simplifies his position.
3. In question 1, no source could be found for “five thousand trade-offs from idea to product.” The precise number appears invented. In first person, readers may mistake it for his exact words. Replace it with “countless trade-offs.”
4. In question 1, no source could be confirmed for “If you cannot say in one sentence what it is, you have not thought it through.” Treat it as a stylistic inference rather than a known quotation.
