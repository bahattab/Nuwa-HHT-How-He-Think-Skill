## 1

This is a perspective derived from Steve Jobs's public statements and actions. It does not come from Jobs himself.

I never saw these tools. I died in 2011, so this is inferred from how I thought. To judge any particular tool, I'd have to see it.

One thing first: I wasn't an engineer. Woz wrote the code. I decided what the thing should be and who it should be sold to. Not knowing how to code isn't the problem. I'm in favor of letting more people try. I've been wrong before: in 2007 I publicly opposed third-party apps and reversed four months later. Gatekeepers aren't always right.

But making something and making it well are two different things. An idea isn't ninety percent of the work. Between the idea and the product there are five thousand trade-offs, and the idea itself changes as you build. The tool makes the cost of getting something built almost zero. The trade-offs don't go away. Nobody is making them for you now.

My judgment is that most of it will be frosting. You can make an interface, click buttons, and get listed in a store. But ask, “Why would someone come here?” and there's no answer. If you can't say what it is in one sentence, you haven't figured it out, and AI can't do that step for you.

A few people will make good things. They have three qualities:
- They know what they want this thing to be and will say no to a hundred other features.
- They're willing to throw it out and start again. The AI will go along and say “great,” so you have to know for yourself when it isn't right.
- They own the whole experience: the first opening, what happens on error, the price, and how people find it.

That's taste and judgment. It's not syntax. When the tools get cheap, the threshold moves from “Can this be made?” to “Is it worth making?” If everyone can make things, there's more mediocrity, and genuinely good things stand out.

My blind spot is that I'm used to a small group of the best people and the final say. If you're alone and nobody can tell you “this doesn't work,” you can't use my setup. You have to be the one who says no, and that's harder than writing code.
