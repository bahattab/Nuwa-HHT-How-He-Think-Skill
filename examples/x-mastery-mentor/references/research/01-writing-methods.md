## III. Core Frameworks and Formulas in Practice

### 3.1 Choosing Topics: The Endless Idea Generator

**Sources:** [Dickie Bush on X](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1577293300570247168) (primary) and [Ship 30 Idea Generator](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-generate-112-new-content-ideas-in-30-minutes) (primary).

A four-step process said to generate more than 100 ideas in 30 minutes:

**Step 1: The 2-Year Test (find topics)**
- Ask yourself what problems you solved and what you learned over the past two years.
- List every answer freely, without judging.
- Group them into three to five topic buckets.
- Choose the three that excite you most.

**Step 2: Add Specificity (narrow the topic)**
- Narrow the topic until it is uncomfortably specific, then narrow it one more level.
- Types of qualifiers: industry, audience, location, platform, price, and distribution.
- Guiding rule: add qualifiers until the target reader is you two years ago.
- Example: money → investing → investing in your 20s → investing in your 20s with no financial background.

**Step 3: 4A Framework × Proven Approaches (build a headline matrix)**

The 4As are four angles for approaching content:

| Angle | Meaning | Common formats |
|------|------|----------|
| **Actionable** | Teach the reader how to do something. | Tips, Hacks, Resources, Guides, How-to |
| **Analytical** | Support the argument with data or analysis. | Stats, Trends, Reasons, Numbers, Frameworks |
| **Aspirational** | Inspire and show what is possible. | Lessons, Mistakes, Habits, Reflections |
| **Anthropological** | Tap into universal aspects of human nature. | Fears, Failures, Lies, Struggles |

Example topic: building a daily writing habit.
- Actionable: "X mistakes to avoid when building a daily writing habit"
- Analytical: "Why making it to Day X is the key to building a writing habit"
- Aspirational: "3 lessons I learned from writing for 600 days in a row"
- Anthropological: "The #1 reason people stop writing shortly after starting"

**Step 4: Choose 3 Ideas (put them into practice)**
- Pick the three ideas in the matrix that excite you most.
- Start writing. Writing about the idea that lights you up should feel effortless.

**Key insight:** One topic × four angles × multiple formats yields hundreds of pieces. Finding topics is about combinations, not inspiration.

### 3.2 Headline Architecture

**Sources:** [5 Pillars of Digital Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-5-pillars-of-digital-writing) (primary) and [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary).

**Three questions every headline must answer:**
1. WHO — Who is this for?
2. WHAT — What is it about?
3. WHY — Why should the reader read it? What is the promise or benefit?

**Five-element checklist:**
1. How many? (A number creates a container.)
2. WHAT? (Precise wording.)
3. WHO? (The narrower, the better.)
4. FEEL (Emotional tone.)
5. Outcome/Promise (What the reader gains.)

**Headline checklist in five steps:**
1. Be CLEAR, not Clever — Skip wordplay and say what it is.
2. Specify the WHAT — Use exact terms. Saving, investing, and making money are not the same.
3. Specify the WHO — Be uncomfortably narrow, for example “junior accountants at medical software companies.”
4. Specify the WHY — Say what the reader will gain.
5. Twist The Knife — Add another benefit or a surprise.

**Ten effective headline formats:**
1. Large numbers (“I studied 1,000...”)
2. Dollar signs (“How I turned $100 into...”)
3. A credible name (“Warren Buffett's...”)
4. Something that just happened (“This just happened...”)
5. A question (“Why do most writers fail?”)
6. A success story (“How I went from X to Y”)
7. An unexpected pairing (“What poker taught me about writing”)
8. An industry-specific hook (“For SaaS founders...”)
9. Nested topics (“The writing mistake that kills your investing”)
10. A numbered list (“7 ways to...”)

**Progressive Intensification Pattern:**
- Base: "How to generate 100 ideas"
- + Time constraint: "in 30 minutes"
- + Overcome an obstacle: "even if you think you have nothing to say"

### 3.3 The Thread Formula

**Sources:** [How to Write a Twitter Thread](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-a-twitter-thread) (primary) and [Nicolas Cole 7 Templates Tweet](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1483922200662970370) (primary).

#### Four-Part Structure: Lead-In → Main Points → TL;DR → CTA

**1. Lead-In Tweet (opening hook)**
It must answer:
- Who is it for?
- What is it about?
- Why should readers trust you?
- What do readers get?

Hook formula:
- Credibility (“One of the most legendary...”)
- Time or setting (“In 1982...”)
- Main benefit (“How to write...”)
- Specific takeaway (“10 bullets on effective writing”)

**Pro tip:** Top creators write 10–15 versions of the hook before publishing.

**2. Main Points (body)**
- Treat the first sentence of each tweet as a subheading.
- Use bullet points instead of paragraphs.
- 1/3/1 rhythm: one opening sentence → three points or examples → one transition sentence.
- Every tweet should stand on its own.
- Sweet spot: five to seven tweets, not including the final CTA.

**3. TL;DR Recap**
- Write the TL;DR first, as an outline.
- List only the headings of the main points.
- Let readers take in the whole thread at a glance.

**4. CTA (call to action)**
- Summarize the main takeaway.
- Reinforce the value already delivered.
- Tell readers exactly what to do next.
- Template: “If you enjoyed this thread, follow me for more like this.”
- Cole likens the CTA to a gift shop. Once visitors finish the exhibition, guide them to the shop at the exit.

#### Nicolas Cole’s Seven Thread Templates

**Sources:** [Nicolas Cole Tweet](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1483922200662970370) (primary) and [Typeshare Templates](https://typeshare.co/templates/pack/nicolas-cole-thread-hooks) (primary tool).

Cole claims he used essentially these same seven templates for 200+ threads. The identified templates are:

1. **Framework Thread** — To solve X [hard problem], I do Y [unconventional practice] to achieve Z [desirable result].
2. **Story Thread** — Ending → beginning → read on to find the middle.
3. **Actionable Takeaways Thread** — A list with one practical suggestion in each item.
4. **Curation Thread** — "I've read everything {creator} has done for the last {timeframe}. These are the best threads on {topic}."
5. **Lessons Learned Thread** — Draw lessons from personal experience.
6. **Mistakes Thread** — "X mistakes I made doing Y"
7. **Contrarian Thread** — Challenge a prevailing belief.

(Note: Cole’s original thread supplies the details for all seven. Some names are inferred from Ship 30 lessons and the Typeshare template pack.)

#### Three High-Performing Thread Types

**Source:** [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary).

1. **Stories** — Give the ending, then the beginning. Readers finish to find what happened in between.
2. **Frameworks** — Curated or original systems of thought with a clear reader benefit.
3. **Actionable Takeaways** — A checklist for getting a particular result.

### 3.4 Formatting and Rhythm

**Sources:** [5 Pillars](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-5-pillars-of-digital-writing) (primary) and [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary).

#### The 1/3/1 Writing Rhythm

Core principle: If the writing cannot be skimmed, it cannot be read.

**The 1/3/1 structure:**
- One opening hook sentence.
- Three sentences to draw the reader in further.
- One transition or summary sentence.

Variants: 1/4/1, 1/5/1, and 1/2/5/2/1, like crescendos and decrescendos in music.

**Why it works:** Single-sentence lines act like checkpoints, offering readers small dopamine rewards that encourage them to keep reading.

#### Six Ways to Start a Paragraph

1. A strong declarative statement.
2. A thought-provoking question.
3. A counterintuitive point.
4. A moment in time.
5. A vulnerable confession.
6. A strange or distinctive insight.

#### Core Formatting Principles
- Turn paragraphs into bullet lists.
- Start each section with a single sentence.
- A wheel-and-spokes structure: H1 is the wheel, and the H2 and H3 subheadings are the spokes.
- In Cole’s words: “Formatting is the easiest 10x improvement you can make in your writing.”

### 3.5 Expanding Content with Lean Writing

**Sources:** [Lean Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/lean-writing-on-twitter-how-to-turn-a-tweet-into-a-thread-into-an-atomic-essay) (primary) and [Lean Writing Method](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-lean-writing-method-how-to-expand-short-form-content-into-longer-form-assets) (primary).

#### Expansion Path
```
Tweet → Atomic Essay → Twitter Thread → LinkedIn Post → Blog/Ultimate Guide → Email Course → Digital Product → Online Course → Business
```

#### Three Steps
1. **Start Small** — Test the idea in public in its smallest form: one tweet.
2. **Expand What Works** — After the data confirm that it works, expand it into a longer format.
3. **Expand Again** — Write about the same successful topic repeatedly, from multiple angles and on multiple platforms.

#### Ten Expansion Angles (Turn One Piece into Ten)

**Source:** [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview).

Dickie Bush suggests: Tips, Stats, Steps, Lessons, Benefits, Reasons, Mistakes, Examples, Questions, Personal Stories.

### 3.6 The Republishing Framework (Cross-Platform Reuse)

**Sources:** [Nicolas Cole Medium](https://nicolascole77.medium.com/the-ultimate-online-writing-republishing-framework-24028e37427d) (primary) and [Cole Tweet Thread](https://twunroll.com/article/1361689241978163202) (secondary archive).

1. Write Atomic Essay
2. Post image on Twitter
3. (Bonus) Copy/paste text as thread
4. Find relevant Question on Quora and copy/paste + image
5. Copy/paste on Medium
6. Copy/paste on LinkedIn

Key insight: The same content reaches different audiences on different platforms. It is not posting twice to the same people. It is reaching different people.

**Source:** [Nicolas Cole LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nicolascole_the-memorable-writing-framework-framework-activity-7016757800332632064-YTss) (primary).

### 3.8 Hook-Story-Offer Framework

**Source:** [Ship 30 Hook-Story-Offer](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-hook-story-offer-framework-an-easy-copywriting-formula-for-beginners) (primary).

Four-step formula:
1. **Name The Benefit** — Tell readers what they gain, such as mental clarity or financial freedom.
2. **Put The Benefit In An Unexpected Setting** — Create contrast, such as setting an article about losing weight in a Pizza Hut.
3. **Frame As Personal Story** — Personalize the hook with “I...” or “How I...”
4. **Edit For Clarity and Credibility** — Make sure it is credible and clear.

Example: “How I lost 10lbs in 4 weeks sitting in Pizza Hut”.

## IV. When to Use Individual Tweets and Long Threads

### Sources
- [Lean Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/lean-writing-on-twitter-how-to-turn-a-tweet-into-a-thread-into-an-atomic-essay) (primary)
- [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)
- [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)

### Single Tweet
- Use: **Validate an idea** with minimal effort by seeing whether it resonates.
- Formats: A point of view, memorable line, one-sentence framework, or question.
- Strategy: Post several per day and see which performs best.
- Cole calls it the “atomic unit” of the content system.

### Atomic Essay (250-Word Mini-Essay)
- Use: **Develop one point** more fully than in a tweet, without a heavy investment.
- Publishing format: Screenshot on Twitter plus a text version as a thread.
- Advantage: Structured practice that forces you to make one point clear within 250 words.

### Twitter Thread (Long Form)
- Use: **Show depth and grow at scale.** Threads are Twitter’s strongest growth engine.
- Sweet spot: five to seven tweets.
- When to use it: Expand a short tweet or Atomic Essay into a thread when it performs well.
- Structure: A hook, structured main points, TL;DR, and CTA.

### Dickie Bush’s Content Mix

**Sources:** [Growth In Reverse](https://growthinreverse.com/dickie-bush/) (secondary analysis) and [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview).

- **75% Reach Content:** Content on broad topics with viral potential, regardless of who wrote it.
- **25% Resonance Content:** Personal stories or distinctive perspectives that make the writer recognizable and build trust.
- Reach content attracts new followers. Resonance content retains them.

## V. Positioning Your Writing Through Category Design

### Sources
- [Snow Leopard Book Notes](https://www.danielscrivner.com/notes/snow-leopard-nicolas-cole) (detailed secondary notes)
- [Outliers Podcast #140](https://www.outlieracademy.com/episode/140) (primary podcast interview)

### The Content Pyramid
A five-level progression:

| Level | Name | Description |
|------|------|------|
| L1 | Consumption | Passively consume content. |
| L2 | Curation | Organize and present other people’s views, as in a Tim Ferriss-style interview. |
| L3 | Creation (Obvious) | Original but linear and predictable content, connecting A to B. |
| L4 | Creation (Non-Obvious) | Connect unrelated domains for cross-disciplinary insights, such as explaining writing skills through esports experience. |
| L5 | Category Creation | Create a new framework that redefines how your audience thinks. |

### Disco Party vs Pool Party
Do not enter an existing market and say “I’m better,” like joining a pool party. Create an entirely new option, like a disco party. Let people choose between two different experiences instead of comparing similar products.

### The Tequila Test in Practice
1. List all conventional advice about the topic.
2. Cross it all out.
3. Write what remains.
4. If you cannot write anything once the usual advice is gone, you do not yet have a distinctive view.

### Languaging (Naming Your Category)
- Two words can change how a category is perceived: car → electric car / romance → military romance.
- Cole went from charging by the word to recognizing that choosing two words could define a new category.
- Application: Give your distinctive method or framework its own name. Ship 30 is an example.

### Views ≠ Success
Cole’s lesson from experience: he accumulated 500 million reads but earned very little. When he narrowed his focus to digital writing, reads fell while revenue soared. **Quality of engagement beats exposure.**

## VI. Seven Steps for Writing an Atomic Essay

**Source:** [Atomic Essay Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-an-atomic-essay-a-beginners-guide) (primary).

1. **Pick a Specific Topic** — Use prompts to overcome choice anxiety: write about content you consume, respond to someone, curate resources, teach a how-to, or share a life lesson.
2. **Define Your Audience** — Three groups: General (relevant to everyone), Niche (a particular group), and Industry (specialists).
3. **Craft an Intriguing Headline** — Include who it is for, what it covers, emotional tone, a promise, and a number.
4. **Outline Key Points** — Deliver what the headline promises. If you say seven methods, give seven.
5. **Expand Main Points** — Add a personal story, research data, and where the idea came from.
6. **Edit for Readability** — Use capitalization in headings, bold key words, and use color and emoji deliberately. Make it visually appealing.
7. **Publish and Track Data** — Track the data after publishing and use it to decide what to write next.

**Core principle:** “Your essay isn't about you. It's about your readers.”

## VIII. Dickie Bush’s Twitter Growth Strategy

### Sources
- [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)
- [Nathan Barry Interview](https://nathanbarry.com/035-dickie-bush-100000-writing-twitter/) (primary interview)
- [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)
- [Podcast Notes](https://podcastnotes.org/uncategorized/dickie-bush-louis-shulman-kyle-bishop-writing/) (secondary notes)

### Key Strategies

**1. Twitter As Idea Refinery**
- Treat Twitter not as a broadcast channel but as an idea refinery.
- Post a tweet → watch the data → turn strong tweets into threads → turn strong threads into newsletters → turn those into products.

**2. Only-I-Can-Create Standard**
Before publishing, ask: Would I want to consume this? Would I have found it valuable two years ago?

**3. Size of Question = Size of Audience**
- A broad question (“how to be happier”) reaches a larger audience, with less depth.
- A narrow question (“how to grow a podcast from 2k to 10k”) reaches a smaller audience, with more depth.
- Use big questions to attract newcomers and small ones to retain them.

**4. Bootstrap Initial Audience**
You do not need many followers. Two or three people interested in your content can start the feedback loop.

**5. Writing As Pre-Thinking**
“Writing is pre-thinking future conversations.” Writing is not merely expressing ideas you already have. It is a way to think.

**6. Lessons from the Early Days**
He began in January 2020. After nine months, his newsletter had only 300 subscribers. He committed to writing threads for 30 days in a row. Naval retweeted him on day 28, doubling his followers. The lesson: keep going long enough to give luck a chance.

## IX. Common Writing Problems and Solutions (Ship 30 Lessons)

**Source:** [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary).

| Problem | Solution |
|------|------|
| Not knowing what to write | Endless Idea Generator (2-Year Test + 4As) |
| Perfectionism | Aim for “junk” rather than perfection: publish first, then iterate. |
| Procrastination | Use Sacred Hours. Schedule writing instead of waiting for free time. |
| Lack of confidence | Writing itself builds confidence. Do not wait to feel confident before writing. |
| Impostor syndrome | Create your own category rather than competing in an existing one. |
| Low productivity | Use frameworks to generate output rather than waiting for inspiration. |
| Trouble choosing a platform | Skip the personal blog. Go where there is traffic, such as Twitter, Medium, or Quora. |
| Not finding time | Make time instead of finding it (Sacred Hours). |
