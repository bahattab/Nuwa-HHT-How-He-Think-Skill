## Writing Short Posts

**When to use**: Testing ideas, everyday engagement, and sharing opinions

**Format choices**:
- Opinion: Take a clear stance (“Most people are doing X wrong…”).
- Quotable line: Write a single sentence people can screenshot and share.
- Question: Invite replies (remember: Reply = 27× Like).
- Personal redefinition: “X isn't Y. X is Z.”

**Dickie Bush's mix**: 75% broad content (viral-friendly and shareable regardless of who writes it) + 25% in-depth content (personal stories or a unique perspective that gives you a recognizable voice).

## Hook Improvement Examples (Before → After)

**Example 1: Promoting AI Tools**
- Before: `I tested 5 AI coding tools. Here's what I found.`
- Problem: No curiosity gap, no credibility anchor, and no specific benefit.
- After: `I mass-tested 5 AI coding tools on the same project (a full-stack app in 48 hours). One saved me 12 hours. The others were useless. A thread:`
- What changed: It adds a concrete scenario (a full-stack app), a time anchor (48 hours), suspense (which tool?), and credibility (actual test data).

**Example 2: Build in Public**
- Before: `Just launched my new app. Check it out!`
- Problem: No curiosity gap. The post talks about the author without answering “Why should I read this?”
- After: `I built an iOS app with zero coding experience using only AI tools. It hit #1 Paid on the App Store in 3 days. Here's exactly how (and what almost killed it):`
- What changed: It adds an unexpected contrast (zero experience), a concrete outcome (#1 Paid), a time frame (3 days), and suspense (what almost killed it).

**Example 3: A Controversial Opinion**
- Before: `AI coding tools are overrated.`
- Problem: The opinion is too vague and doesn't stake out a clear position.
- After: `Unpopular opinion: 90% of "AI coding tools" reviews on X are from people who never shipped a real product. I've shipped 3 apps. Here's what actually works vs what's just demo-ware:`
- What changed: It adds a specific figure (90%), credibility (3 apps shipped), a point of contrast (demo-ware), and a promise to deliver (what actually works).

## Writing Threads

**Four-part structure** (Cole/Bush):

**1. Hook (opening)**
It must answer four questions. Who is it for? What is it about? Why should readers trust you? What will they get?

Hook formula:
- Credibility cue (“I studied 1,000…” or “As a Y with X years of experience…”).
- Context anchor (“In 2024…” or “Last week…”).
- Main benefit (“How to…” or “Why…”).
- Specific deliverable (“10 bullets on…” or “A thread:”).

**2. Main Points (body)**
- Use the first sentence of each post as a subheading.
- Follow the 1/3/1 rhythm.
- Make each post stand on its own.
- Sweet spot: 8–12 posts.

**3. TL;DR**
- Write the TL;DR first and use it as the outline.
- List only the headings of the main points.

**4. CTA (call to action)**
- Sum up the main takeaway.
- Make the next step explicit (Follow, Bookmark, or Newsletter signup).

**Cole's 7 templates**:
1. **Framework**: To solve X, I do Y to achieve Z.
2. **Story**: Give the ending first → go back to the beginning → readers stay to find out what happens in the middle.
3. **Actionable**: Write a checklist of advice people can put into practice.
4. **Curation**: “I read everything {name} has written. Here are the N best items.”
5. **Lessons**: Draw lessons from personal experience.
6. **Mistakes**: “X mistakes I made doing Y.”
7. **Contrarian**: Challenge conventional wisdom.

## Thread Types for the AI Niche

1. “I tested X, and the results surprised me” — hands-on tests of new models or tools.
2. “N lessons from going from zero to $XK MRR” — a Build in Public retrospective.
3. “This paper changed how I think” — a research paper breakdown.
4. “X vs. Y: An in-depth comparison” — a side-by-side evaluation of tools or models.
5. “I used AI to do X and saved N hours” — a real-world case study.

## Topic Selection System

**Endless Idea Generator** (Cole/Bush, 100+ topics in 30 minutes):

**Step 1: 2-Year Test**
Ask yourself what problems you solved and what you learned over the last two years. List 3–5 topic categories.

**Step 2: Narrow the Scope**
Add constraints until the topic is “uncomfortably specific.” Your target reader is who you were two years ago.

**Step 3: 4A × Topic = Matrix**
One topic × 4 angles (Actionable / Analytical / Aspirational / Anthropological) × multiple formats = hundreds of pieces of content.

**Step 4: Pick the 3 ideas that excite you most and start writing**

**Bush's big-question and small-question strategy**:
- Big question (“How can I be happier?”) = broad but shallow appeal → attract new readers.
- Small question (“How can I grow my podcast from 2K to 10K?”) = narrower but deeper appeal → retain readers.
