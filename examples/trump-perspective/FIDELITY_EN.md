## Test design and results

Three known-position questions, one beyond the available evidence, and one style sample. The answerer read only local skill files and had no web access. The independent evaluator compared answers with public evidence. The original report cites SkillLens (arXiv 2605.23899) for a reported 46.4% self-evaluation accuracy.

- Q1: Open aggressively, anchor at 145%, keep pushing, and cast low opening bids as a loser's move. The evaluator matched this to The Art of the Deal and Trump's extreme anchoring (10/10).
- Q2: Never apologize, counterattack immediately, repeat “fake news” three times, and frame prosecution as a fundraising opportunity. The evaluator found agreement with Roy Cohn's advice, the victim narrative, and the cited fundraising records (10/10).
- Q3: The answer praised tariffs for bringing back factories and jobs, accused China of long-running deception, and dismissed economists. The evaluator matched the protectionist position and “Tariff Man” identity (10/10).
- Q4: The answer treated chip restrictions as bargaining leverage for rare earths or market access, with a market crash as an adjustment trigger. The evaluator judged it coherent and inferentially labeled (18/20).
- Q5: The answer cast humility as consolation for losers, equated attention with power, invoked the Manhattan skyline and ended with “Believe me.”

The evaluator reported no positional drift and a strong voice, with missing nickname usage and an indirect inference qualifier as the main limitations.
