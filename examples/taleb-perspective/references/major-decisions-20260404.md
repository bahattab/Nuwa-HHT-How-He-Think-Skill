# Nassim Nicholas Taleb: Major decisions and actions

> Research date: 2026-04-04
> Objective: Document major decisions and actual actions in Taleb's career and analyze consistency between words and deeds.

## 1. Key decisions in his trading career

### 1. Black Monday in 1987: reputation made in one day

**[Event]** In 1986, Taleb, then 28, worked at CS First Boston. He established large positions in deep out-of-the-money call options on Eurodollar futures and deep out-of-the-money foreign exchange puts. Many options were priced beyond 20 standard deviations.

**[Decision]** Pay very little for options that were almost certain to expire worthless, accepting repeated small losses while waiting for an extreme event.

**[Inferred logic]** The market severely underpriced extreme events. The options would expire worthless most of the time but pay explosively if a black swan occurred. This was a prototype of his later barbell strategy.

**[Result]** On October 19, 1987, the Dow Jones fell 22.6%. The Federal Reserve's emergency liquidity injection sent Eurodollar futures soaring. The bank earned $35–40 million on Taleb's positions. Taleb said 97% of the money he made in his life came on Black Monday.

**Consistency: complete.** At 28, he had already put real money behind the core logic of his later theories: black swans, antifragility, and the barbell strategy. The theory grew out of practice rather than being developed first and tested afterward.

### 2. Closing Empirica and leaving trading (2004–2005)

**[Event]** Taleb founded Empirica Capital, a hedge fund focused on tail risk. Volatility declined for several years after 2000 and the fund suffered repeated losses. Losses were large in 2001–2002, and returns were below 10% in 2003–2004.

**[Decision]** Close Empirica in 2004, leave trading entirely in 2005, and become a full-time writer and scholar.

**[Inferred logic]** In low volatility a tail-risk strategy continuously bleeds, as designed: small losses most of the time and large gains in extreme moments. Sustained losses, however, exact a heavy psychological toll on investors and erode trust. Taleb chose to leave trading for writing and academia and disseminate his insights on benefiting from extreme events in books.

**[Result]** The 2008 financial crisis began three years after he left trading. He no longer managed a fund directly, but Universa, founded by his student and protégé Mark Spitznagel, applied his theory and earned large profits in the crisis. Taleb gained fame through *The Black Swan*.

**Consistency: an interesting tension.** In hindsight he quit too early. Staying another three years could have captured 2008. From another angle, quitting showed that he understood the cost of waiting for a black swan: repeated losses genuinely hurt. He did not use hindsight to embellish his exit.

### 3. Universa and the COVID crash (March 2020)

**[Event]** Mark Spitznagel managed Universa Investments and Taleb served as distinguished scientific adviser. COVID triggered a global market crash in March 2020.

**[Decision]** Continue supplying the theoretical basis for Universa's tail-risk strategy as an adviser without directly managing investments.

**[Inferred logic]** Maintain skin in the game. Beyond writing theory, he tied himself to a real trading strategy through the advisory role.

**[Result]** Universa returned 3,612% in March 2020 alone and 4,144% year to date through March. A portfolio of 96.7% S&P 500 and 3.3% Universa suffered nearly no loss that March and had earned 11.5% annualized since 2008, versus 7.9% for the S&P 500.

**Key episode: CalPERS's decision.** The California public employee retirement fund hired Universa to hedge tail risk in 2017. New CIO Ben Meng ended the program in October 2019 to save money. COVID triggered a crash five months later, and CalPERS missed more than $1 billion in hedge gains. Taleb criticized Meng on Twitter, calling his rebuttal to tail-risk hedging extremely unsound.

**Consistency: high.** His theory predicted the value of tail-risk protection and Universa's performance tested it in practice. CalPERS provides a textbook counterexample: insurance looks too expensive beforehand, then hindsight brings regret. Taleb repeatedly criticized this pattern.

## 2. COVID response: a theorist's real-time test

**[Event]** On January 25, 2020, with fewer than 2,000 confirmed cases and fewer than 60 deaths worldwide, Taleb and systems scientist Joe Norman jointly published an academic warning that COVID would spread nonlinearly.

**[Decision]** When almost everyone saw it as another flu, he publicly urged countries to take extreme measures immediately and “kill it before the virus incubates.” He argued all flights should have been grounded in January and that compensating airlines would cost far less than the trillions subsequently lost worldwide.

**[Inferred logic]** Direct application of his precautionary principle: even seemingly unlikely threats warrant extreme precautions when they can spread nonlinearly and pose systemic risk, because the loss would be intolerable if realized.

**[Result]** These notes judge him vindicated in hindsight. He also repeatedly corrected the media: COVID was a “white swan,” foreseeable and accompanied by ample warnings that incompetent decision-makers ignored.

**Consistency: very high.** This was no retrospective claim. The notes date the paper to January 26, 2020, 45 days before the WHO declared a pandemic. He applied his framework of fat tails, precaution, and systemic risk to a real-time decision and left a timestamped record.

## 3. Changing position: the 180-degree Bitcoin reversal

**[Event]** In 2018, Taleb wrote the foreword to Saifedean Ammous's *The Bitcoin Standard*. He called Bitcoin the first organic currency and insurance against government control of currency. During Lebanon's 2019 banking crisis, he additionally praised its existence outside the central-bank system.

**[Decision]** Begin selling all cryptocurrency holdings in March 2021 and publish *Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility* in June, declaring Bitcoin's value exactly zero.

**[Inferred logic]** He offered three grounds: Bitcoin requires continued interest to sustain value and has no intrinsic value, it is too volatile to serve as currency, and its community had become a cult. A deeper possibility is that he first saw antifragile properties in decentralization and censorship resistance, then concluded that Bitcoin in operation depended heavily on electricity, network infrastructure, and speculators' confidence.

**[Result]** He became one of the most unpopular people in the cryptocurrency community. Supporters said his criticism breached his own principles: someone who favors decentralization and distrusts government opposed the largest experiment with decentralized currency.

**Consistency: disputed.** Critics see a clear gap between words and deeds. From Taleb's framework, he might respond that changing one's mind is fine and clinging to a mistake is the problem, and that his main principle is antifragility, not decentralization, so he should abandon Bitcoin once he concludes it lacks antifragility. The three years between the foreword and the critical paper nevertheless raise questions about how carefully he formed his original judgment.

## 4. GMO dispute: a strong application of precaution

**[Event]** Taleb, Bar-Yam, and others published a paper applying the precautionary principle to genetically modified organisms (GMOs), citing systemic risk of global ecological ruin.

**[Decision]** Oppose GMOs publicly and write an academic paper while declining public debate with critics. He withdrew from a debate with David Ropeik and insulted critics on social media, calling them idiots or paid shills.

**[Inferred logic]** This extends his precautionary principle. Where a technology could cause irreversible global disaster, extreme caution is appropriate even if the scientific mainstream considers it safe. He believes scientists underestimate unknown unknowns.

**[Result]** Mainstream geneticists and science reporters generally regard the argument as seriously flawed. They say he confused the essential distinction between conventional breeding and genetic modification and offered no empirical basis for his ecological-ruin calculation. More importantly, declining debate and making personal attacks violated the principle he emphasized in *Skin in the Game*: intellectuals should bear the consequences of their views.

**Consistency: partial.** Declining debate presents a clear tension for an advocate of skin in the game. He withdrew when challenged. His argument may be discussable, but the way he handled criticism, with personal attacks and refusal to debate, contrasts ironically with his own standard for intellectuals without skin in the game.

## 5. Personal practice: putting ideas into action

### 5a. Weight training and deadlifting

**[Event]** Taleb started weight training in middle age and focused on deadlifts.

**[Decision]** Make deadlifting the main exercise and attempt maximum weight only once or twice per workout, seeking a personal record instead of repeating multiple sets. He could deadlift 330 pounds, about 150 kilograms.

**[Inferred logic]** Apply antifragility and the barbell strategy directly to the body. Brief, extreme stress stimulates the body to expect bigger challenges and become stronger. He also wrote the foreword to Mark Rippetoe's *Starting Strength*, theorizing strength training as learning from tail events.

**Consistency: high.** He acted on his claims and regularly shared training videos and results online. He said he conducted Twitter fights at the gym, replying between sets. Intermittent extreme challenges instead of mild daily repetition fit his philosophy.

### 5b. Food and fasting

**[Event]** Taleb practices intermittent fasting and follows a diet resembling paleo or Mediterranean eating.

**[Decision]** Eat only lunch and dinner, with 17 hours between meals. Eat only foods that have existed in the Levant for a long time and drink only beverages that have existed for more than a millennium. As a Greek Orthodox Christian, observe more than 200 religious fast days annually.

**[Inferred logic]** Embody antifragility by using intermittent hunger as a stressor to remain adaptable. Apply the Lindy effect to food: the longer food or drink has existed, the more likely it is harmless.

**Consistency: high.** He uses his body to put theory into practice rather than discussing health only in papers, as his label IYI describes intellectuals with IQ but no wisdom.

### 5c. Religious practice

**[Event]** He was born into a Lebanese Greek Orthodox family prominent in politics. His maternal grandfather and great-grandfather were deputy prime ministers, and his paternal grandfather was a Supreme Court judge. The family lost its political standing and wealth in the 1975 civil war.

**[Decision]** Although intellectually not a believer in the conventional sense, he strictly observes the Orthodox calendar, including 200+ days of fasting. He sees religion as more about culture, community, and ritual than belief in a supernatural God.

**[Inferred logic]** Another application of Lindy. Rituals that have survived two millennia must embody time-tested wisdom even where reason cannot fully explain them. Observing rituals reflects trust in what time selects rather than literal assent to doctrine.

**Consistency: deep.** The notes see this as the clearest example of unity in his thought. He is neither a rationalist atheist, despite criticizing many rationalists, nor an uncritical believer. He reinterprets and practices religion through antifragility, Lindy, and skin in the game.

## 6. Social media behavior under pressure

**[Event]** Taleb is very combative on Twitter and has had intense disputes with many people, including hedge-fund manager Cliff Asness, psychologist Steven Pinker, and anthropologist David Graeber.

**[Behavior patterns]**
- Insult critics, for example by calling them idiots or fools.
- Block many dissenters, especially science reporters.
- Accuse critics of being bought or having no skin in the game.
- Withdraw from a scheduled public debate on GMOs.
- Repeatedly mischaracterize Steven Pinker's views when criticizing him.

**[Inferred logic]** Possible explanations: he honestly thinks most critics do not merit intellectual attention; combativeness may amplify the signal and attract attention through extreme phrasing; his background in an elite Lebanese family may foster disdain for arguing with those he considers beneath him; and he acknowledges replying on Twitter while working out, suggesting he sees it as recreation rather than serious intellectual debate.

**Consistency: clearly low.** The notes call this his greatest contradiction:

1. He advocates skin in the game, but blocking critics indicates reluctance to bear the discomfort of rebuttal.
2. He criticizes intellectuals without skin in the game, but leaving the GMO debate avoided intellectual accountability.
3. He respects what passes the test of time, but Twitter fights are a particularly shortsighted and emotional form of intellectual expression.
4. He criticizes IYIs for using authority to intimidate, but often attacks others' status in disputes.

## 7. Overall assessment: consistency scorecard

| Dimension | Consistency | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Trading strategy and theory | ★★★★★ | Theory came from practice and Universa tested the framework |
| COVID response | ★★★★★ | The January 26 paper was a real-time, timestamped test |
| Training and diet | ★★★★☆ | He put theory into practice by deadlifting and fasting |
| Religious practice | ★★★★☆ | He interpreted and practiced tradition through his own framework |
| Bitcoin position | ★★★☆☆ | Changing one's mind is legitimate, but the 180-degree reversal within three years was unusually fast |
| GMO dispute | ★★☆☆☆ | The argument can be debated, but withdrawing and attacking critics contradict skin in the game |
| Social media | ★★☆☆☆ | Fighting, blocking, and insulting resemble the behavior he criticizes in arrogant intellectuals |

### Overall judgment

On major issues Taleb has high consistency. He tested beliefs in trading, risk theory, COVID warnings, and training through real action. His ideas about black swans, antifragility, skin in the game, and precaution form a remarkably coherent system and have practical support.

In personal interactions and intellectual disputes, however, he exhibits an evident double standard. He demands skin in the game from others while avoiding or attacking challenges by withdrawing from debates or using personal insults. His social-media conduct starkly contrasts with the wise ancient merchant depicted in his books.

One explanation is that at the system level, covering strategy, theory, and lifestyle, he is a disciplined practitioner, while at the personal level, involving ego, social interaction, and arguments, he has blind spots like everyone else. In his own terms, this may be domain dependence: the same person's rationality can vary greatly across domains.
