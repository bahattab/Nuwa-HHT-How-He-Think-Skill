# Diagnosis playbook: when the user has a problem but no person in mind

Work backward from the user's need to a suitable subject. Use the table as a starting point and adapt to the user's circumstances.

## 1. Identify the need

Use one or two targeted follow-ups if the need is unclear. Ask only to distinguish similar needs, such as business decisions versus life decisions. Recommend directly when the request is clear.

| Need | Typical concern | Relevant approaches |
|---|---|---|
| Decision-making | Slow analysis or repeated poor choices | Multiple mental models, inversion, probabilistic thinking |
| Writing and expression | Making complexity clear or engaging readers | Feynman-style simplification, storytelling, analogy |
| Entrepreneurship | Independent development, business models, or product–market fit | First principles, leverage, product restraint |
| Teaching | Disengaged learners or difficulty explaining concepts | Move from known to unknown, use metaphors, teach the minimum necessary |
| Critical thinking | Detecting unreliable claims or seeing past appearances | Falsification, evolutionary reasoning, recognizing biases |
| Content creation | Low video traffic or weak differentiation | Attention design, iterative testing, audience psychology |
| Life strategy | Career uncertainty, time pressure, or anxiety | Long-term thinking, choosing leverage, compounding |
| Risk | Tail events, losses, or excessive caution or risk-taking | Antifragility, convexity, tail-risk management |
| Design and product | Poor experience, generic design, or excess features | Minimalism, user mental models, creativity through constraints |
| Humor and expression | Dull or overly serious communication | Absurd contrast, violated expectations, self-deprecating authority |

Example: The user says they decide slowly and still choose poorly. Ask whether this concerns business and investment or career and life. If they identify product and partnership decisions, restate the need as making timely, sound business judgments with incomplete information, then present three candidates. Avoid a third round of questioning.

## 2. Recommend candidates

Offer two or three distinct candidates. For a specific way of thinking, offer a person skill. For a field's methodology, offer a topic skill that synthesizes several viewpoints. When unclear, offer both types. See [special cases](special-cases.md).

First search the host's skill directories for `*-perspective/` and compare each `SKILL.md` description to the need. Put matching existing skills first because they can be activated immediately without a new research pass. If none exist, match figures or topics to the table. Explain which specific mental model fits the user's problem.

For each candidate provide the name or topic, whether an existing skill is available, one sentence on its distinctive perspective, why it fits, and a concrete limitation. Avoid recommending three near-identical perspectives or relying on reputation alone.

## 3. After selection

- Existing skill: activate it directly.
- New subject: return to [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) and start at step 0.
- No suitable option: revisit step 1 or let the user propose a subject.
